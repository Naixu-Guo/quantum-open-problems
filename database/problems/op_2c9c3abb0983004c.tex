\section{Uniformly efficient HSW pretty-good decoding}
\label{sec:problem-78}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Can Holevo--Schumacher--Westmoreland codebooks operating at every rate below
their ensemble Holevo information be chosen so that their square-root, or
pretty-good, measurements have uniform quantum
implementations whose cost is polynomial in the blocklength and the logarithm
of the codebook size?  Fix an efficiently implementable finite-dimensional
memoryless channel $\mathcal N:A\to B$ and an efficiently preparable ensemble
$\{p(x),\phi_x^A\}_{x\in\mathcal X}$.  For
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{XB}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p(x)|x\rangle\!\langle x|_X
    \otimes\mathcal N(\phi_x),
  \qquad
  \chi(\mathcal N,p):=I(X;B)_\omega,
  \label{eq:p78-holevo-information}
\end{equation}
fix a rate $0<R<\chi(\mathcal N,p)$ and choose a fixed typicality parameter
$\delta>0$ sufficiently small as a function of the gap
$\chi(\mathcal N,p)-R$.  A length-$n$ codebook consists of
$M_n$ words $x^n(m)$, with $n^{-1}\log_2M_n\to R$, and corresponding output
states
\begin{equation}
  \rho_m^{B^n}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal N(\phi_{x_i(m)}),
  \qquad m\in\{1,\ldots,M_n\}.
  \label{eq:p78-codeword-states}
\end{equation}
Let $\Pi_{\bar\rho,\delta}^{(n)}$ be the typical projector of
$\bar\rho^B:=\sum_x p(x)\mathcal N(\phi_x)$, and let
$\Pi_{m,\delta}^{(n)}$ be the conditionally typical projector for the
codeword $x^n(m)$ and the product state in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-codeword-states}.  The detection operators used in the
standard HSW decoder are
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{m,n}
  :=\Pi_{\bar\rho,\delta}^{(n)}
      \Pi_{m,\delta}^{(n)}
      \Pi_{\bar\rho,\delta}^{(n)},
  \qquad
  G_n:=\sum_{m=1}^{M_n}\Gamma_{m,n}.
  \label{eq:p78-hsw-detection-operators}
\end{equation}
Using the Moore--Penrose inverse on $\operatorname{supp}G_n$, their
square-root measurement, including its failure outcome, is
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{m,n}:=G_n^{-1/2}\Gamma_{m,n}G_n^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \Lambda_{0,n}:=I-\sum_{m=1}^{M_n}\Lambda_{m,n}.
  \label{eq:p78-hsw-pgm}
\end{equation}

The codebook must be succinct rather than an explicit list of exponentially
many words.  Require uniform polynomial-size coherent circuits that (i)
compute $x_i(m)$, (ii) prepare a purification $|\psi_m\rangle_{B^nR_n}$ of
$\rho_m^{B^n}$ coherently in $m$, and (iii) give a controlled block encoding
of the operators in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-hsw-detection-operators}.  Denote these
circuits by $C_n$, $O_n$, and $U_{\Gamma,n}$, so that
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    C_n|m,i,0\rangle&=|m,i,x_i(m)\rangle,\\
    O_n|m,0\rangle&=|m\rangle|\psi_m\rangle,
      \qquad \operatorname{Tr}_{R_n}|\psi_m\rangle\!\langle\psi_m|=\rho_m,\\
    (\langle0|_Z\otimes I)U_{\Gamma,n}(|0\rangle_Z\otimes I)
      &=\sum_{m=1}^{M_n}|m\rangle\!\langle m|\otimes\Gamma_{m,n}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:p78-access-model}
\end{equation}
The promise in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-access-model} includes efficient inverse
circuits and a classical algorithm that outputs their gates in time
polynomial in $n$ and $\log M_n$.

The target is a uniform decoder circuit producing a POVM
$\{\widetilde\Lambda_{j,n}\}_{j=0}^{M_n}$ whose output distribution, averaged
over transmitted messages, approximates that of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-hsw-pgm}.  For every $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, require
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_n
  :=\frac{1}{2M_n}\sum_{m=1}^{M_n}\sum_{j=0}^{M_n}
    \left|
      \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
        (\widetilde\Lambda_{j,n}-\Lambda_{j,n})\rho_m^{B^n}
      \right]
    \right|
  \leq\varepsilon,
  \label{eq:p78-average-implementation-error}
\end{equation}
with gate and oracle complexity
$\operatorname{poly}(n,\log M_n,\log(1/\varepsilon))$, rather than polynomial
in $M_n$ or in the inverse of an exponentially small singular value.  Taking,
for example, an inverse-polynomial sequence $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_n\to0$,
the implemented decoder must retain vanishing average message error.  The
problem is to construct such codebooks and decoders for every instance in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-holevo-information}, or to prove that this uniform target is
impossible under the access model in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-access-model}.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Section~20.3 of Wilde's text explicitly identifies efficient implementation
of the collective HSW square-root measurement as an open problem and notes
that a direct sequential implementation may examine exponentially many
messages \sourcecite{ref:p78-wilde}{Wil17}.  The statement above makes the
previously implicit input model and complexity target explicit.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Gily\'en, Lloyd, Marvian, Quek, and Wilde give a quantum algorithm for
  a general pretty-good instrument using purified access, block encodings,
  quantum singular-value transformation, and oblivious amplitude
  amplification.  In a PGM specialization, the environment dimension is the
  number of hypotheses $M_n$, and the query bounds also depend on inverse
  spectral parameters of the relevant aggregate and hypothesis operators.
  These factors can be exponential for an HSW codebook, so the result does
  not give the polynomial target following
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-average-implementation-error}
  \sourcecite{ref:p78-gilyen-et-al}{GLM+22}.

  \item The same work proves black-box lower bounds for generic Petz-map
  implementation by reduction from unstructured search.  Those bounds show
  that dimension and condition dependence cannot simply be removed in that
  oracle model, but they are not an HSW-specific lower bound and do not prove
  that Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-average-implementation-error} is impossible.  A
  positive solution would have to exploit the memoryless-channel and
  succinct-codebook structure in
  Eqs.~\eqref{eq:p78-codeword-states}--\eqref{eq:p78-access-model}
  \sourcecite{ref:p78-gilyen-et-al}{GLM+22}.

  \item Behera, Granda Arango, Sergioli, and Giuntini propose a
  support-aware, threshold-regularized pseudoinverse and demonstrate hybrid
  block-encoding and circuit-level realizations.  Their 2026 preprint relies
  on classical spectral preprocessing and small-dimensional numerical
  demonstrations; it does not prove a uniform asymptotic complexity bound for
  exponentially large HSW codebooks
  \sourcecite{ref:p78-behera-et-al}{BGS+26}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The unresolved requirement is polynomial scaling in the natural succinct
input length of an HSW family operating below its ensemble Holevo information.
Existing PGM algorithms are
efficient when their oracle, dimension, and condition parameters are
efficient, but that statement alone does not control an HSW codebook with
$M_n=2^{\Theta(n)}$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:p78-average-implementation-error}
uses an ensemble-average metric so that exponentially small spectral
subspaces need not be reproduced on irrelevant inputs.  The target is
specifically the typical-projector HSW measurement in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p78-hsw-pgm}, not the raw-state PGM of the codeword density
operators.
