\section{All-code exponential strong converse for degradable channels}
\label{sec:problem-77}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Does every finite-dimensional degradable quantum channel satisfy an all-code
exponential strong converse for quantum communication at its quantum capacity?
Let $V:A\to B\otimes E$ be a Stinespring isometry, and define the channel and
one complementary channel by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal N(\rho):=\operatorname{Tr}_E(V\rho V^\dagger),
  \qquad
  \mathcal N^c(\rho):=\operatorname{Tr}_B(V\rho V^\dagger).
  \label{eq:p77-complementary-channels}
\end{equation}
The channel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-complementary-channels} is degradable when
there is a completely positive trace-preserving map
$\mathcal D:\mathcal L(B)\to\mathcal L(E)$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal N^c=\mathcal D\circ\mathcal N.
  \label{eq:p77-degradability}
\end{equation}
For a channel satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-degradability}, its quantum
capacity is the single-letter coherent information
\begin{equation}
  Q(\mathcal N)=Q^{(1)}(\mathcal N)
  :=\max_{\rho_A}
    \left[S(\mathcal N(\rho_A))-S(\mathcal N^c(\rho_A))\right],
  \qquad
  S(\tau):=-\operatorname{Tr}(\tau\log_2\tau).
  \label{eq:p77-degradable-capacity}
\end{equation}

For an arbitrary $n$-use entanglement-transmission code, let the encoder and
decoder be CPTP maps
$\mathcal E_n:\mathcal L(S_n)\to\mathcal L(A^{\otimes n})$ and
$\mathcal R_n:\mathcal L(B^{\otimes n})\to\mathcal L(\widehat S_n)$, with
$\dim S_n=\dim\widehat S_n=M_n$.  If
$\Phi_{M_n}^{R_nS_n}$ is maximally entangled, define the code rate and
entanglement fidelity by
\begin{equation}
  r_n:=\frac{1}{n}\log_2 M_n,
  \qquad
  F_n:=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
    \Phi_{M_n}^{R_n\widehat S_n}
    \bigl(\operatorname{id}_{R_n}\otimes
      \mathcal R_n\circ\mathcal N^{\otimes n}\circ\mathcal E_n\bigr)
      (\Phi_{M_n}^{R_nS_n})
  \right].
  \label{eq:p77-code-fidelity}
\end{equation}
The all-code exponential strong converse asks whether, for every
$R>Q(\mathcal N)$, there are constants $\gamma_R>0$ and $n_R$ such that every
code in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-code-fidelity} obeys
\begin{equation}
  r_n\geq R,\qquad n\geq n_R
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  F_n\leq 2^{-\gamma_R n}.
  \label{eq:p77-exponential-strong-converse}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:p77-exponential-strong-converse} requires a bound for every
encoder--decoder pair, rather than for almost every member of a random code
ensemble.

\subsection*{Status}

Solved

\subsection*{Source}

Morgan and Winter explicitly isolated the upgrade from their pretty-strong
converse to a full strong converse for degradable channels
\sourcecite{ref:p77-morgan-winter}{MW14}.  Wilde records this gap, together
with the corresponding all-code gap for the quantum erasure channel, in
Section~24.10 of his text \sourcecite{ref:p77-wilde}{Wil17}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Morgan and Winter proved that rates above
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-degradable-capacity} force the asymptotic fidelity to be
  bounded away from one for every degradable channel.  Their ``pretty
  strong'' converse did not force $F_n$ to zero and therefore did not imply
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-exponential-strong-converse}
  \sourcecite{ref:p77-morgan-winter}{MW14}.

  \item Wilde and Winter proved exponential fidelity decay above the quantum
  capacity of the erasure channel for almost all codes drawn from their
  random ensemble.  The exceptional set was not excluded, so their theorem
  was not the all-code statement in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-exponential-strong-converse}
  \sourcecite{ref:p77-wilde-winter}{WW14}.

  \item Kondra, Brinster, Kampermann, Bru{\ss}, and Wyderka prove
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p77-exponential-strong-converse} for every finite-dimensional
  degradable channel and also prove an exponential strong converse for every
  finite-dimensional antidegradable channel.  Since every quantum erasure
  channel is degradable or antidegradable, their result gives the first
  all-code exponential strong converse for the erasure channel over its full
  parameter range \sourcecite{ref:p77-kondra-et-al}{KBK+26}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The solved status records the theorem claimed in
\sourcecite{ref:p77-kondra-et-al}{KBK+26}.  As of 2026-09-02, that work was
a one-month-old, unrefereed v1 preprint, so the closure is provisional and
should be re-audited if the preprint changes.
Problem~\ref{sec:problem-55} asks
for an exponential strong converse for the larger, potentially strict class
of transpose-degradable channels; the theorem above applies to ordinarily
degradable and antidegradable channels and does not by itself settle that
extension.
