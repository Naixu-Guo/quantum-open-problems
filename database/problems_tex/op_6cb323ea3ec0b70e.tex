\section{Trace-exponential lower bound for matrix-word averages}
\label{sec:problem-34}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Is the normalized trace average of all words in two positive-definite matrices
always bounded below by the corresponding trace exponential?  Let
$A,B\in M_d(\mathbb C)$ be positive definite, let $n,m\geq1$, and let
$\mathcal W_{n,m}$ be the set of words containing exactly $n$ letters $A$ and
$m$ letters $B$.  Define the normalized average by
\begin{equation}
  p_{n,m}(A,B)
  :=\frac{1}{\binom{n+m}{n}}
    \sum_{W\in\mathcal W_{n,m}}\operatorname{Tr}W(A,B).
  \label{eq:p34-word-average}
\end{equation}
The question is whether the quantity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-word-average}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  p_{n,m}(A,B)
  \geq \operatorname{Tr}\exp\!\bigl(n\log A+m\log B\bigr)
  \label{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound}
\end{equation}
for every finite $d$ and all $n,m\geq1$.  A positive-semidefinite extension
is obtained, whenever the limit exists, by applying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound} to $A+\varepsilon I$ and
$B+\varepsilon I$ and then taking $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Cha and Lee formulate a two-sided refined BMV inequality and disprove only
its upper half, leaving the lower trace-exponential comparison stated here
open \sourcecite{ref:p34-cha-lee}{CL26}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Equality holds in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound} when
  $A$ and $B$ commute.  If $n=1$ or $m=1$, cyclicity makes every summand in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-word-average} equal to
  $\operatorname{Tr}(A^nB^m)$, and the Golden--Thompson inequality proves
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound}
  \sourcecite{ref:p34-golden}{Gol65},
  \sourcecite{ref:p34-thompson}{Tho65}.  Thus the first unresolved range has
  $n,m\geq2$.

  \item The proved Bessis--Moussa--Villani coefficient-positivity theorem
  implies only $p_{n,m}(A,B)\geq0$, which is weaker than
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound}
  \sourcecite{ref:p34-lieb-seiringer}{LS04},
  \sourcecite{ref:p34-stahl}{Sta13}.

  \item The original refinement also proposed the distinct upper comparison
  $\operatorname{Tr}(A^nB^m)\geq p_{n,m}(A,B)$.  Cha and Lee disproved that
  comparison with $3\times3$ positive-semidefinite matrices at $n=m=5$ and
  obtained an unbounded ratio
  $p_{5,5}(A,B)/\operatorname{Tr}(A^5B^5)$
  \sourcecite{ref:p34-cha-lee}{CL26}.  Their construction does not disprove
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound}; Dinh's subsequent pinching
  proposal also concerns a replacement for the failed upper comparison
  \sourcecite{ref:p34-dinh}{Din26}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Cha and Lee state the two-sided refinement explicitly and disprove only its
upper half.  The lower comparison in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p34-trace-exponential-bound} has neither a general proof nor a
counterexample.
