\section{Many-copy Bell nonlocality of every entangled state}
\label{sec:op_0e0ceaac739b74fd}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Does every entangled bipartite state become Bell nonlocal under collective
local measurements on some finite number of copies?  Fix an integer $d\geq2$
and a state $\rho$ on $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$.  At blocklength $n$,
Alice and Bob choose arbitrary collective positive operator-valued measurements
$\{M_{a|x}\}_a$ and $\{N_{b|y}\}_b$ on their respective $n$ subsystems.
Settings $x,y$ and outcomes $a,b$ range over finite sets, and
\begin{equation}
  p_\rho^{(n)}(a,b\mid x,y)
  :=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho^{\otimes n}(M_{a|x}\otimes N_{b|y})\right].
  \label{eq:0e0c-behavior}
\end{equation}
A behavior is Bell local when it admits a local hidden-variable decomposition
\begin{equation}
  p(a,b\mid x,y)=\int\mu(d\lambda)\,p_A(a\mid x,\lambda)\,p_B(b\mid y,\lambda),
  \label{eq:0e0c-local}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is a probability measure independent of the settings and
$p_A,p_B$ are local response distributions.  For $s\geq2$, let
$\mathsf L^{(s)}$ be the set of states on $\mathbb C^s\otimes\mathbb C^s$ all
of whose behaviors, for all finite setting and outcome sets and all local
positive operator-valued measurements, are Bell local.  Let
$\mathsf L_\infty^{(d)}$ be the set of states $\rho$ for which every behavior
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-behavior} admits a decomposition as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-local} for every finite $n$; equivalently,
$\rho^{\otimes n}\in\mathsf L^{(d^n)}$ for all $n\geq1$, with Alice's $n$
subsystems grouped against Bob's.  Let $\mathrm{SEP}_d$ denote the separable
states on $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$.  No communication, auxiliary
entangled state, or postselection is allowed, and every measurement outcome is
retained.  The question is whether
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf L_\infty^{(d)}=\mathrm{SEP}_d
  \qquad\text{for every finite }d\geq2.
  \label{eq:0e0c-question}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Cavalcanti, Ac\'in, Brunner, and V\'ertesi explicitly ask, in the discussion
of their results, whether every entangled state is a nonlocal resource when a
sufficient number of copies is considered \sourcecite{ref:0e0c-cabv}{CABV13}.
\v{S}upi\'c, Skrzypczyk, and Cavalcanti state in Sec.~I that whether every
entangled state can be superactivated in this way is open
\sourcecite{ref:0e0c-ssc}{SSC17}.  Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-question} is a precise
formulation of that question.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Palazuelos proves that Bell nonlocality can be superactivated by tensor
  powers without local preprocessing: certain isotropic states that admit a
  local hidden-variable model for all local positive operator-valued
  measurements have tensor powers violating a Bell inequality based on the
  Khot--Vishnoi game \sourcecite{ref:0e0c-pal}{Pal12}.  Thus
  \begin{equation}
    \rho\in\mathsf L^{(d)},
    \qquad
    \rho^{\otimes n}\notin\mathsf L^{(d^n)}
    \label{eq:0e0c-superactivation}
  \end{equation}
  is possible for some states $\rho$ and finite $n$.  Existence of the effect
  in Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-superactivation} is settled; its universality over
  entangled states is the question.

  \item Cavalcanti, Ac\'in, Brunner, and V\'ertesi prove the sufficient condition
  \begin{equation}
    F_d(\rho)>\frac1d
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \rho\notin\mathsf L_\infty^{(d)},
    \label{eq:0e0c-fraction-criterion}
  \end{equation}
  where
  \begin{equation}
    F_d(\rho):=\max_{U\in\mathrm U(d)}
    \langle\Phi_d|(I_d\otimes U)\rho(I_d\otimes U^\dagger)|\Phi_d\rangle,
    \qquad
    |\Phi_d\rangle:=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|jj\rangle,
    \label{eq:0e0c-fraction}
  \end{equation}
  is the fully entangled fraction and $\mathrm U(d)$ is the unitary group
  \sourcecite{ref:0e0c-cabv}{CABV13}.  Their proof twirls $\rho$ into an
  isotropic state with the same value of Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-fraction} and
  applies Khot--Vishnoi Bell inequalities to collective local measurements on
  $\rho^{\otimes k}$; the twirling unitaries are absorbed into the local
  measurements, so no filtering, auxiliary state, or postselection is used.
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-fraction-criterion} covers every entangled isotropic
  state, but not every entangled state: the authors note that it does not
  apply to some distillable states.

  \item \v{S}upi\'c, Skrzypczyk, and Cavalcanti distinguish the many-copy scenario
  from quantum networks, hidden nonlocality with local preprocessing, and
  Bell tests with quantum inputs, in which every entangled state can be
  detected \sourcecite{ref:0e0c-ssc}{SSC17}.  The unresolved implication is
  \begin{equation}
    \rho\notin\mathrm{SEP}_d
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \exists\,n\geq1:\ \rho^{\otimes n}\notin\mathsf L^{(d^n)},
    \label{eq:0e0c-implication}
  \end{equation}
  not a scenario in which a referee supplies additional quantum inputs.

  \item Renner, Lobo, Konderak, Augusiak, and Ac\'in prove a stronger many-copy
  statement for pure states: for every pure entangled state $|\psi\rangle$
  there is an integer $k$ such that suitable local measurements on
  $|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}$ give a behavior with zero local content (Result~2
  in Sec.~V of \sourcecite{ref:0e0c-rlkaa}{RLKAA26}).  The local content of a
  nonsignaling behavior $p$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \operatorname{LC}(p):=\max\left\{q\in[0,1]:\
    p=q\,p_L+(1-q)\,p_{\mathrm{NS}},\
    p_L\ \text{Bell local},\ p_{\mathrm{NS}}\ \text{nonsignaling}\right\}.
    \label{eq:0e0c-local-content}
  \end{equation}
  Pure entangled states are already Bell nonlocal for a single copy, as
  recalled in \sourcecite{ref:0e0c-pal}{Pal12}; the conclusion
  $\operatorname{LC}(p)=0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-local-content} strengthens
  the pure-state case and does not address mixed states.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

No proof or counterexample to Eq.~\eqref{eq:0e0c-question} is known.  A
counterexample must be an entangled state with a local hidden-variable model
for every finite tensor power and all collective local measurements, not
merely a state that passes finitely many Bell tests.  This many-copy property
is not a regularized rate, so activation by copies of the same state is
meaningful here.  Activation with auxiliary states, local filtering, networks,
or quantum inputs concerns different scenarios.  Literature checked through 15 September 2026.
