\section{Exact spectral gap of random Pauli rotations}
\label{sec:op_aaf9791beced84e4}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Is the exact Haar-mixing spectral gap of uniformly random Pauli rotations equal to $2^n(2^n-3)/(8(4^n-1))$ for every $n\geq4$?

Let $\mathcal P_n=\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}\setminus\{I^{\otimes n}\}$. One step chooses $P\in\mathcal P_n$ and $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ uniformly and applies $e^{i\theta P}$. On $L^2(\mathrm{SU}(2^n),\mathrm{Haar})$, define

\begin{equation}
(K_nf)(U)=\mathbb E_{P,\theta}f(e^{i\theta P}U),
\qquad
\Delta_n=1-\|K_n|_{L^2_0}\|_{2\to2},
\label{eq:aaf9-1}
\end{equation}

where $L^2_0$ is the zero-Haar-mean subspace. The conjectured value of the gap in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-1} is

\begin{equation}
\Delta_n=\frac{2^n(2^n-3)}{8(4^n-1)}
\label{eq:aaf9-2}
\end{equation}

Does Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-2} hold for every $n\geq4$?

\subsection*{Status}

Solved

\subsection*{Source}

Baer and Haah posed this question as Conjecture 3.48 in the first version of their preprint. Their Definition 1.1 takes the gap over all finite-dimensional unitary representations of $\mathrm{SU}(2^n)$. \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-baer26}{Baer26}

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item For $n\geq3$, Baer and Haah's Theorem 3.16 and Proposition 3.17 give
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{4^n+16}{16(4^n-1)}\leq\Delta_n\leq\frac{2^n(2^n-3)}{8(4^n-1)}.
    \label{eq:aaf9-3}
  \end{equation}
  The upper bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-3} comes from an eigenvalue in the balanced fourth moment $U^{\otimes4}\otimes\overline U^{\otimes4}$. \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-baer26}{Baer26}

  \item Baer and Haah determined the gap for $n\leq3$, including $\Delta_3=5/63$. At four qubits their bounds gave
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{15}\leq\Delta_4\leq\frac{26}{255}.
    \label{eq:aaf9-4}
  \end{equation}
  Their July 2026 preprint conjectured that the upper bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-3} was attained for every $n\geq3$. \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-baer26}{Baer26}

  \item Yuxuan Zhang gave a four-qubit counterexample. In $\bigwedge^8\mathbb C^{16}$, the sum of the basis wedges for the $30$ affine hyperplanes of $\mathbb F_2^4$, each in ascending binary order, is an eigenvector of the averaged rotation with eigenvalue $14/15$. The corresponding mean-zero function on $\mathrm{SU}(16)$ gives $\Delta_4\leq1/15$. The lower bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-4} then gives $\Delta_4=1/15$. \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-zhang26}{Zha26} (\href{https://github.com/Naixu-Guo/quantum-open-problems/issues/122}{GitHub \#122}).

  \item Dong and Fan subsequently determined both gaps. Writing $d=2^n$, their Theorem 3.1 gives
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      \Delta_{\mathrm{SU}(d)} &= \frac{d-8}{8(d-1)} && (n\geq4),\\
      \Delta_{\mathrm{PU}(d)} &= \frac{d(d-3)}{8(d^2-1)} && (n\geq3).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:aaf9-exact-gaps}
  \end{equation}
  The projective gap in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-exact-gaps} is attained in the balanced fourth moment. \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-dong-fan26}{DF26}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The answer is no. At four qubits the Haar-mixing gap on $\mathrm{SU}(16)$ is $1/15=17/255$, strictly below the $26/255$ predicted by Eq.~\eqref{eq:aaf9-2}. The central element $e^{i\pi/8}I$ acts by $-1$ on Zhang's eigenvector and by $+1$ on every balanced tensor moment. This explains how the projective walk can have the larger gap $26/255$. \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-zhang26}{Zha26} \sourcecite{ref:aaf9-dong-fan26}{DF26}
