\section{Tough error models}
\label{sec:op_b1b41f737f9b4aeb}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Determine $c(e,n)$ and construct tough error models in the sense of
\sourcecite{ref:p14-krueger-werner}{KW05}.  Let
$H=\mathbb{C}^n$, and let an $e$-dimensional error model be a complex linear
subspace $E\subseteq\operatorname{End}(H)$ with $1\le e\le n^2$.  A linear
subspace $C\subseteq H$ corrects $E$ exactly when, for every $A,B\in E$, there
is a scalar $\lambda(A,B)\in\mathbb{C}$ such that
\begin{equation}
  P_C A^\dagger B P_C=\lambda(A,B)P_C,
  \label{eq:p14-error-correction-condition}
\end{equation}
where $P_C$ is the orthogonal projector onto $C$.  Define the guaranteed code
dimension by
\begin{equation}
  c(e,n)
  :=\min_{\substack{E\subseteq\operatorname{End}(H)\\
                    \dim E=e}}
    \max\left\{
      \dim C:
      \begin{array}{l}
        C\subseteq H\text{ is a linear subspace, and}\\
        \forall A,B\in E\ \exists\lambda(A,B)\in\mathbb{C}:\
        P_C A^\dagger B P_C=\lambda(A,B)P_C
      \end{array}
    \right\}.
  \label{eq:p14-guaranteed-code-dimension}
\end{equation}
Determine $c(e,n)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p14-guaranteed-code-dimension}, exactly
or with asymptotically matching bounds, and exhibit explicit tough error
models whose largest correcting code is close to this value.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Kr\"uger and Werner explicitly introduce the extremal function $c(e,n)$ and
ask for tough error models attaining its scale
\sourcecite{ref:p14-krueger-werner}{KW05}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The compression condition in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p14-error-correction-condition} is the Knill--Laflamme
  criterion for perfect correction of the linear error space $E$
  \sourcecite{ref:p14-knill-laflamme-viola}{KLV00}.

  \item The Knill--Laflamme--Viola existence argument and the projection
  construction recorded in the source give the ceiling-sensitive bounds
  \begin{equation}
    \left\lceil
      \frac{\left\lceil n/e^2\right\rceil}{e^2+1}
    \right\rceil
    \le c(e,n)
    \le\left\lceil\frac{n}{e}\right\rceil.
    \label{eq:p14-known-bounds}
  \end{equation}
  The upper bound uses an error space spanned by nearly equal-rank orthogonal
  projections.  The parameter gap is polynomial in $e$
  \sourcecite{ref:p14-knill-laflamme-viola}{KLV00},
  \sourcecite{ref:p14-krueger-werner}{KW05}.

  \item The one-error case can be settled exactly.  For
  $E=\operatorname{span}\{A\}$, the condition is a scalar compression of the
  Hermitian matrix $A^\dagger A$.  The Hermitian higher-rank numerical-range
  formula therefore gives
  \begin{equation}
    c(1,n)=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil.
    \label{eq:p14-one-error-exact}
  \end{equation}
  A positive matrix with simple spectrum makes the bound sharp
  \sourcecite{ref:p14-choi-et-al}{CKZ06}.  At the other extreme, the upper
  construction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p14-known-bounds}, together with the trivial
  one-dimensional code, gives $c(e,n)=1$ for $e\ge n$.

  \item Weaver's quantum Turán framework interprets scalar compressions as
  quantum anticliques
  \sourcecite{ref:p14-weaver}{Wea19}.  Allen and Kornell's improved
  low-dimensional anticlique theorem applies to
  $\mathcal{V}_E:=\operatorname{span}(\{I\}\cup
  \{A^\dagger B:A,B\in E\})$.
  Since $\dim\mathcal{V}_E\le5$ when $e=2$, it gives
  $c(2,n)\ge\lceil n/9\rceil$, improving the historical lower bound of
  order $n/20$
  \sourcecite{ref:p14-allen-kornell}{AK25}.  This remains far from the upper
  bound $\lceil n/2\rceil$; general operator-system estimates also do not
  exploit all of the product structure in $\mathcal{V}_E$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The source calls an error model $E$ ``tough'' when the largest code correcting
$E$ has dimension close to $c(e,n)$
\sourcecite{ref:p14-krueger-werner}{KW05}.  The unresolved task is to close
the bounds in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p14-known-bounds} for $2\le e<n$ and construct
such models at the correct scale.  Results for structured Pauli noise or
arbitrary operator systems do not determine
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p14-guaranteed-code-dimension}.
