\section{Real four-quhex absolutely maximally entangled state}
\label{sec:op_f60b9a99d7945e3b}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Does there exist an $\operatorname{AME}(4,6)$ state whose coefficients are
real in a product basis?  Fix $[6]=\{0,\ldots,5\}$ and ask for a normalized
state
\begin{equation}
  |\psi\rangle
  =\sum_{i,j,k,l\in[6]}T_{ijkl}|i\rangle_A|j\rangle_B
    |k\rangle_C|l\rangle_D,
  \qquad
  T_{ijkl}\in\mathbb{R},
  \qquad
  \sum_{i,j,k,l\in[6]}T_{ijkl}^{2}=1 .
  \label{eq:p41-real-state}
\end{equation}
The state in \eqref{eq:p41-real-state} must have maximally mixed reductions
on every pair of parties: for each $S\subseteq\{A,B,C,D\}$ with $|S|=2$,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_S:=\operatorname{Tr}_{S^{c}}|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
  =\frac{I_{36}}{36} .
  \label{eq:p41-ame-condition}
\end{equation}

Equivalently, define the $36\times36$ flattening $U$ and its reshuffling
$U^R$ and second-factor partial transpose $U^\Gamma$ by
\begin{equation}
  U_{(i,j),(k,l)}:=6T_{ijkl},
  \qquad
  (U^R)_{(i,k),(j,l)}:=U_{(i,j),(k,l)},
  \qquad
  (U^\Gamma)_{(i,j),(k,l)}:=U_{(i,l),(k,j)} .
  \label{eq:p41-rearrangements}
\end{equation}
Here the three matrices in \eqref{eq:p41-rearrangements} are the coefficient
flattenings for the bipartitions $AB|CD$, $AC|BD$, and $AD|CB$, up to the
displayed ordering of tensor factors.  Consequently,
\eqref{eq:p41-ame-condition} is equivalent to the orthogonal
$2$-unitarity conditions
\begin{equation}
  U\in O(36),
  \qquad
  U^R\in O(36),
  \qquad
  U^\Gamma\in O(36) .
  \label{eq:p41-orthogonal-two-unitarity}
\end{equation}
Thus the problem is also to construct an orthogonal $2$-unitary matrix of
order $36$, or prove that none exists.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Rajchel-Mieldzio\'c et al. explicitly ask whether a real
$\operatorname{AME}(4,6)$ state, equivalently an orthogonal $2$-unitary
matrix of order $36$, exists \sourcecite{ref:p41-review}{RBR+26}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Rather, Burchardt, Bruzda, Rajchel-Mieldzio\'c, Lakshminarayan, and
  \.{Z}yczkowski constructed an exact
  $\operatorname{AME}(4,6)$ state, equivalently a complex $2$-unitary matrix
  of order $36$.  Its entries use nontrivial twentieth-root phases.  Their
  numerical searches found high-entangling-power orthogonal matrices but no
  orthogonal $2$-unitary, leading them to conjecture nonexistence in $O(36)$;
  this is numerical evidence, not a proof
  \sourcecite{ref:p41-rather}{RBB+22}.

  \item Cha proved, for the generalized-Pauli stabilizer convention used in
  that work, that no stabilizer $\operatorname{AME}(4,6)$ state exists.
  Hence any real solution of \eqref{eq:p41-ame-condition} must lie outside
  that stabilizer class, but the theorem does not exclude real
  nonstabilizer states \sourcecite{ref:p41-cha}{Cha26}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Item T2 of \sourcecite{ref:p41-review}{RBR+26} explicitly retains the real
$\operatorname{AME}(4,6)$ problem and its orthogonal $2$-unitary formulation.
The precise unresolved gap is whether the three simultaneous orthogonality
conditions in \eqref{eq:p41-orthogonal-two-unitarity} have any real solution;
neither the known complex constructions nor the stabilizer obstruction
answers this question.
