\section{Universal purification with classically simulable operations}
\label{sec:op_a64dc63d6ae49127}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Can classically simulable operations purify an unknown depolarized pure
state from any number of copies?  Fix a dimension $d$ that is either $2$
or odd.  For an unknown pure state $\psi=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ on
$\mathbb C^d$ and a noise parameter $0<\delta<1$, consider the depolarized
copy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal D_\delta(\psi):=(1-\delta)\psi+\delta\,\frac{\mathbb 1_d}{d},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Tr}\bigl[\psi\,\mathcal D_\delta(\psi)\bigr]
  =1-\frac{d-1}{d}\,\delta.
  \label{eq:a64d-noisy-copy}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal A_2$ be the set of completely stabilizer-preserving maps on
qubits and, for odd $d$, let $\mathcal A_d$ be the set of completely
positive-Wigner-preserving maps on qudits; both classes are efficiently
classically simulable, and trace-nonincreasing (probabilistic) members are
allowed.  For $n\geq2$ copies and a success probability $0<s\leq1$, the
optimal Haar-averaged purification fidelity is
\begin{equation}
  F^{\mathcal A_d}_{\delta}(n,s)
  :=\sup\Bigl\{\frac1s\int d\psi\,
    \operatorname{Tr}\bigl[\psi\,
      \mathcal E\bigl(\mathcal D_\delta(\psi)^{\otimes n}\bigr)\bigr]
    :\ \mathcal E\in\mathcal A_d,\
    \int d\psi\,\operatorname{Tr}\,
      \mathcal E\bigl(\mathcal D_\delta(\psi)^{\otimes n}\bigr)=s
  \Bigr\},
  \label{eq:a64d-fidelity}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E$ maps the $n$ copies to one $d$-dimensional system and
$d\psi$ is the Haar measure on pure states.  Is
\begin{equation}
  F^{\mathcal A_d}_{\delta}(n,s)=1-\frac{d-1}{d}\,\delta
  \label{eq:a64d-no-go}
\end{equation}
for every $n\geq2$, every $0<\delta<1$, and every $0<s\leq1$, so that no
classically simulable protocol, deterministic or postselected, improves on
the single-copy fidelity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-noisy-copy}?

\subsection*{Status}

Solved

\subsection*{Source}

He, Zhu, Yao, Liu, Li, and Wang prove Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-no-go} for two
copies and explicitly conjecture that universal purification is impossible
without non-stabilizer resources for every dimension and every number of
copies \sourcecite{ref:a64d-he-et-al}{HZY+26}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Yuxuan Zhang gives a counterexample to Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-no-go}
  for qubits using five copies at $\delta=2/3$. Choose one of the six signed
  Pauli axes uniformly and measure four inputs in that basis. If all four
  outcomes match the chosen sign, prepare that Pauli eigenstate; otherwise
  retain the untouched fifth input. Pauli measurements, stabilizer
  preparation, retention, discarding, and classical randomization make
  this a trace-preserving, completely stabilizer-preserving map, including
  in the presence of reference systems. Its fidelity is at least
  $11693/17496=2/3+29/17496$ for every pure target. Its Haar fidelity gives
  \begin{equation}
    F^{\mathcal A_2}_{2/3}(5,1)\geq\frac{325}{486}
    =\frac23+\frac1{486}>\frac23.
    \label{eq:a64d-zhang-counterexample}
  \end{equation}
  Thus Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-zhang-counterexample} refutes the archived
  universal equality with success probability one. Discarding extra inputs
  and independently accepting the output extend the same conditional
  fidelity bound to every $n\geq5$ and $0<s\leq1$ at this noise strength
  \sourcecite{ref:a64d-zhang-counterexample}{Zha26}, pp.~2--3.

  \item For two copies the no-go statement is a theorem: for qubits under
  completely stabilizer-preserving maps and for every odd $d$ under
  completely positive-Wigner-preserving maps,
  $F^{\mathcal A_d}_{\delta}(2,s)=1-\frac{d-1}{d}\delta$ for every
  $0<s\leq1$, so neither deterministic nor postselected classically
  simulable processing improves the Haar-averaged fidelity
  \sourcecite{ref:a64d-he-et-al}{HZY+26}.

  \item Zhang also gives the four-copy postselection map with Kraus
  operator $K=|0\rangle\langle0000|$. At $\delta=1/2$, its Haar-averaged
  success probability is $s=121/1280$ and its conditional Haar fidelity is
  $547/726=3/4+5/1452>3/4$. This uses the ratio of integrals in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-fidelity}: it improves the success-weighted average,
  not every individual target. The deterministic five-copy construction
  above has a strict fidelity gain for every target without postselection
  \sourcecite{ref:a64d-zhang-counterexample}{Zha26}, p.~3.

  \item The arXiv version of He et al. reports numerical no-improvement
  results for
  \begin{equation}
    (d,n)\in\{(2,3),(2,4),(3,3),(3,4)\},
    \label{eq:a64d-numerics}
  \end{equation}
  in arXiv:2504.10516v2, Table~1
  \sourcecite{ref:a64d-he-et-al}{HZY+26}. The four-copy qubit assertion
  in Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-numerics}, under that version's displayed
  success-weighted fidelity definition, is contradicted by Zhang's
  four-copy example; the manuscript does not establish the cause of the
  numerical discrepancy \sourcecite{ref:a64d-zhang-counterexample}{Zha26},
  pp.~3--4. These numerical claims do not affect the analytical two-copy
  theorem or the five-copy counterexample.

The final journal supplement, Appendix F, restricts its qubit scans to $0<\delta<1-1/\sqrt3$, with noise and success-probability steps of $0.01$. Zhang's four-copy example at $\delta=1/2$ and five-copy example at $\delta=2/3$ lie outside that sampled noise range \sourcecite{ref:a64d-he-et-al}{HZY+26}.

  \item Without the restriction to classically simulable maps, projecting
  the $n$ copies of a depolarized qubit onto their symmetric subspace is
  the optimal universal purifier and raises the fidelity above the
  single-copy value. This unrestricted result does not determine the
  optimal fidelity attainable with classically simulable maps
  \sourcecite{ref:a64d-cirac-ekert-macchiavello}{CEM99}.

  \item He, Xiong, and Wang report two-copy resource laws for a feasible
  Haar-averaged fidelity and success-probability pair $(f,s)$ in the
  purification regime $f\geq\lambda_0=1-(d-1)\delta/d$. Theorem 1 gives an
  exact linear law in $f-\lambda_0$ for exponentiated purification mana in
  odd dimensions; the logarithmic mana includes the normalization
  $-\log_2 s$. Theorem 2 bounds the minimum of
  $s^{-1}\mathcal R(J^{\mathcal E}/d^2)$ over feasible successful
  trace-nonincreasing maps by two linear functions of $f-\lambda_0$ for
  $d=2^m$. Here $J^{\mathcal E}$ is the unnormalized Choi operator and
  $\mathcal R$ is stabilizer robustness. The bounds coincide for $d=2$
  (Appendix D, Eqs. (S79)--(S81)). These preprint results concern depolarizing
  noise and two input copies; they do not determine the attainable fidelity for arbitrary $n$
  \sourcecite{ref:a64d-resource-law}{HXW26}.

  \item Historical GitHub report (2026-09-18): Naixu-Guo reported two-copy universal-purification resource bounds from the He--Xiong--Wang preprint. \href{https://github.com/Naixu-Guo/quantum-open-problems/pull/83}{Pull request \#83}.

  \item Historical GitHub report (2026-09-24): Yuxuan Zhang (GitHub: yuxuanzhang1995) reported a candidate five-copy stabilizer-purification counterexample and a separate four-copy postselection example. \href{https://github.com/Naixu-Guo/quantum-open-problems/issues/108}{Issue \#108}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Solved by counterexample to the universally quantified equality
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-no-go}: the admissible qubit instance in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-zhang-counterexample} suffices to refute it
\sourcecite{ref:a64d-zhang-counterexample}{Zha26}.
The cited resolving result is a working manuscript archived on Zenodo;
it is not presented as a peer-reviewed journal publication. The
analytical two-copy no-go theorem remains unaffected. Zhang's result
does not determine optimal fidelities, the minimum copy number for a
deterministic gain for every target, or the odd-dimensional cases.
The completely positive-Wigner-preserving class in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:a64d-fidelity} is defined only for odd $d$, so even
dimensions above two remain outside the archived formulation.
