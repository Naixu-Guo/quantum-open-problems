\section{Endpoint quantum KKL inequality for Boolean observables}
\label{sec:op_dccbfd9860e08b89}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Does the Montanaro--Osborne $L^2$ quantum KKL inequality hold for every Boolean quantum observable?

For a Boolean function $f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$ under the uniform measure, define

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)=\Pr[f(x)\ne f(x^{\oplus i})].
\label{eq:dccb-1}
\end{equation}

The Kahn–Kalai–Linial theorem implies, for a universal $c>0$,

\begin{equation}
\max_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)
\ge c\,\operatorname{Var}(f)\frac{\log n}{n}.
\label{eq:dccb-2}
\end{equation}

The quantum Boolean-function framework and its proposed KKL extension were introduced by Montanaro and Osborne. \sourcecite{ref:dccb-montanaro08}{Montanaro08}

In that framework, let $n\ge2$ and let $A$ act on $(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}$, with

\begin{equation}
A=A^\dagger,\qquad A^2=I.
\label{eq:dccb-3}
\end{equation}

Write $\tau(A)=2^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}A$. Let $\mathcal E_i$ completely depolarize qubit $i$ while leaving all other tensor factors unchanged:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_i(A)=\frac{I_i}{2}\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_i A,
\label{eq:dccb-4}
\end{equation}

where tensor factors are restored to their original order. Set

\begin{equation}
D_iA=A-\mathcal E_i(A),\qquad
\operatorname{Inf}^{(2)}_i(A)=\tau\big[(D_iA)^\dagger D_iA\big].
\label{eq:dccb-5}
\end{equation}

Conjecture: A universal $c>0$ satisfies

\begin{equation}
\max_i\operatorname{Inf}^{(2)}_i(A)
\ge c\bigl(1-\tau(A)^2\bigr)\frac{\log n}{n}
\label{eq:dccb-6}
\end{equation}

for every such $A$. The particularly clean balanced case imposes $\tau(A)=0$. Jiao et al., Conjecture 1.4, state the displayed variance-weighted form and attribute the underlying squared-influence conjecture to Montanaro and Osborne. It is not a generic statement about bounded observables. \sourcecite{ref:dccb-montanaro08}{Montanaro08}\sourcecite{ref:dccb-jiao24}{Jiao24}

For a diagonal $A=\sum_x f(x)|x\rangle\langle x|$, one has $D_if=(f(x)-f(x^{\oplus i}))/2$. Squaring gives the bit-flip indicator, so this formulation reduces exactly to classical influence.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:dccb-1}, \eqref{eq:dccb-2}, \eqref{eq:dccb-3}, \eqref{eq:dccb-4}, \eqref{eq:dccb-5}, \eqref{eq:dccb-6}.

\subsection*{Source}

The question is explicitly posed or retained as open in the cited primary literature \sourcecite{ref:dccb-montanaro08}{Montanaro08}\sourcecite{ref:dccb-jiao24}{Jiao24}.  The statement is rewritten here to make its hypotheses and success criterion self-contained.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Rouzé, Wirth, and Zhang proved important quantum Talagrand, KKL-type, and Friedgut results using geometric or $L^p$ influences with $p<2$. Those results do not resolve the displayed endpoint. Jiao, Lin, Luo, and Zhou explicitly preserve this distinction and state that the Montanaro–Osborne conjecture remains open. \sourcecite{ref:dccb-rouz24}{Rouz24}, \sourcecite{ref:dccb-jiao24}{Jiao24}

  \item The February 17, 2026 revision by Chang and Li develops further variance-decay and higher-order inequalities. Its relevant influence results remain sub-$L^2$; their constants do not give the required endpoint by simply taking $p\to2$. \sourcecite{ref:dccb-chang26}{Chang26}

  \item An August 6, 2026 paper by Slote, Volberg, and Zhang provides another useful comparison. Its Hermitian-dilation family has small influences on the data qubits but an ancilla of influence one. Section 7 explicitly explains why this is not a counterexample to standard quantum KKL, which counts every qubit. \sourcecite{ref:dccb-slote26}{Slote26}
\end{itemize}
