\section{Uniform modified log-Sobolev constant for one-dimensional Gibbs samplers}
\label{sec:op_92fc12b81d55a704}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Do normalized one-dimensional Chen--Kastoryano--Gily'en Gibbs samplers have a positive modified log-Sobolev constant uniformly in system size at every fixed finite temperature?

For a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian $H$, write

\begin{equation}
\rho_\beta(H)=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z_H(\beta)},\qquad
Z_H(z)=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-zH}),\qquad \beta>0.
\label{eq:92fc-1}
\end{equation}

The temperature is $T=1/\beta$, in units with Boltzmann’s constant equal to one. Relative entropy uses natural logarithms.

“Fixed finite temperature” allows constants to depend badly on $\beta$ but not on the number of sites.

Fix finite $\beta$, on-site dimension $d$, and a one-dimensional finite-range interaction class with bounded local strength. Let $\mathcal L_{H,\beta}=\sum_a\mathcal L_a$ be the Chen--Kastoryano--Gilyén generator specified in Eqs.~(2.15)--(2.22) of \sourcecite{ref:92fc-bergamaschi25}{Bergamaschi25}, using all single-site Pauli jumps, Gaussian filter $\gamma_G$ from their Eq.~(2.20), and width $\sigma=\beta^{-1}$. This fixes the time normalization; the rates may not be rescaled by a factor that grows with system size.

Does there exist $\alpha_\beta>0$, independent of chain length $n$ and of the Hamiltonian in this normalized class, such that

\begin{equation}
D\!\left(e^{t\mathcal L_{H,\beta,*}}(\sigma)
\middle\|\rho_\beta(H)\right)
\leq e^{-2\alpha_\beta t}
D\!\left(\sigma\middle\|\rho_\beta(H)\right)
\label{eq:92fc-2}
\end{equation}

for every state $\sigma$ and $t\geq0$?

Equivalently, in this convention, ask whether

\begin{equation}
\inf_{n,H}\alpha_{\mathrm{MLSI}}(\mathcal L_{H,\beta})>0.
\label{eq:92fc-3}
\end{equation}

The entropy-production form is

\begin{equation}
-\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
\mathcal L_{H,\beta,*}(\sigma)
\bigl(\log\sigma-\log\rho_\beta(H)\bigr)
\right]
\geq2\alpha_\beta D(\sigma\|\rho_\beta(H)).
\label{eq:92fc-4}
\end{equation}

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:92fc-1}, \eqref{eq:92fc-2}, \eqref{eq:92fc-3}, \eqref{eq:92fc-4}.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Bergamaschi and Chen explicitly leave the uniform modified log-Sobolev question open for this one-dimensional sampler family \sourcecite{ref:92fc-bergamaschi25}{Bergamaschi25}. The statement fixes a normalization and rewrites the question self-containedly.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Published provenance and progress. Bergamaschi and Chen prove a system-size-independent spectral gap for the relevant one-dimensional noncommuting family, and obtain polylogarithmic-depth Gibbs preparation by a different, quasi-adiabatic route \sourcecite{ref:92fc-bergamaschi25}{Bergamaschi25}, revised January 23, 2026. Their discussion explicitly leaves the corresponding log-Sobolev implication open. High-temperature rapid-mixing results such as \sourcecite{ref:92fc-rouz26}{Rouz26} do not cover arbitrary fixed finite temperature.

  \item Critical update: Gao and Guo’s September 14, 2026 preprint establishes positive complete MLSI for finite-dimensional CKG samplers through a coercivity criterion \sourcecite{ref:92fc-gao26}{Gao26}. Therefore, mere positivity for each fixed finite system is no longer the question retained here.

  \item Why the September result does not close this formulation. The comparison in \sourcecite{ref:92fc-gao26}{Gao26} includes an index of the asymptotic conditional expectation. For a primitive semigroup with stationary state $\rho$, the order constant

\begin{equation}
C(E)=\inf\{C:\sigma\leq C\rho\ \text{for every density matrix }\sigma\}
\label{eq:92fc-5}
\end{equation}

satisfies, directly,

\begin{equation}
C(E)=\lambda_{\min}(\rho)^{-1}\geq d^n.
\label{eq:92fc-6}
\end{equation}

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:92fc-5}, \eqref{eq:92fc-6}.

  \item Thus a lower bound divided by this quantity does not establish a uniform many-body constant. Moreover, an ancillary-dimension-independent “complete” inequality is not automatically independent of the physical system size.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Status: Retained as open only in the uniform-in-$n$ form. A global reset channel is not an admissible substitute for the specified quasi-local dynamics. The distinction between spectral gap, entropy contraction, and circuit preparation is essential.
