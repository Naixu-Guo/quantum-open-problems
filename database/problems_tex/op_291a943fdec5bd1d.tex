\section{Squashed entanglement of the qubit depolarizing channel}
\label{sec:op_291a943fdec5bd1d}

\paragraph{Problem.}
What is the squashed entanglement $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ of the qubit
depolarizing channel, defined for a mixing parameter $p\in[0,1]$ by
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_p(\rho):=(1-p)\,\rho+p\,\mathrm{Tr}[\rho]\,\frac{I}{2}
  =\bigl(1-\tfrac{3p}{4}\bigr)\rho
  +\tfrac{p}{4}\bigl(X\rho X+Y\rho Y+Z\rho Z\bigr),
  \label{eq:sd-depolarizing-channel}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ ranges over qubit states, $I$ is the qubit identity, and
$X,Y,Z$ are the Pauli matrices, so that the total Pauli error probability,
read off from the second expression in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-depolarizing-channel},
is $\tfrac{3p}{4}$?  Let $U:A'\to BE$ be an isometric extension of
$\Lambda_p$, let $\varphi_{RA'}$ range over pure bipartite input states, and
let $S:E\to E'$ range over completely positive trace-preserving maps of
arbitrary finite output dimension.  For
$\omega_{RBE'}:=(\mathrm{id}_R\otimes(S\circ U))(\varphi_{RA'})$ the squashed
entanglement of the channel is
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)
  :=\max_{\varphi_{RA'}}\ \frac{1}{2}\inf_{S}I(R;B|E')_\omega,
  \label{eq:sd-squashed-entanglement}
\end{equation}
where $I(R;B|E')_\omega$ is the conditional mutual information and all
logarithms are base 2 \sourcecite{ref:sd-takeoka-guha-wilde}{TGW14}.  The
quantity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-squashed-entanglement} is additive over tensor
products of channels and upper-bounds the two-way-assisted quantum and
private capacities, $Q_2(\Lambda_p)\le E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ and
$P_2(\Lambda_p)\le E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$
\sourcecite{ref:sd-takeoka-guha-wilde}{TGW14}; this is its operational role,
since an exact value would be a single-letter upper bound on secret and
quantum transmission over depolarizing noise.  Determine
$E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ as an explicit function of $p$ on $[0,1]$.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

The question is implicit in the defining paper of Takeoka, Guha, and Wilde,
which upper-bounds the depolarizing channel within a restricted squashing
family, describes that bound as not particularly tight, and leaves the exact
value uncomputed \sourcecite{ref:sd-takeoka-guha-wilde}{TGW14}.  Goodenough,
Elkouss, and Wehner later determine the only known exact value, that of the
erasure channel, and record that finding exact channel squashed entanglement
remains hard in general
\sourcecite{ref:sd-goodenough-elkouss-wehner}{GEW16}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Takeoka, Guha, and Wilde define the quantity in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-squashed-entanglement}, prove its additivity over tensor
  products and the bounds $Q_2\le E_{\mathrm{sq}}$ and
  $P_2\le E_{\mathrm{sq}}$, and evaluate for Pauli channels a restricted
  family of squashing maps built from Bell-pair measurements.  For the
  channel of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-depolarizing-channel} the optimizing parameter
  in that family is indicated only numerically, the resulting bound is
  described as not particularly tight, and $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$
  itself is left uncomputed \sourcecite{ref:sd-takeoka-guha-wilde}{TGW14}.

  \item The state measure underlying Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-squashed-entanglement} is the
  squashed entanglement of Christandl and Winter, an additive entanglement
  monotone that vanishes on separable states and never exceeds the
  entanglement of formation \sourcecite{ref:sd-christandl-winter}{CW04}.
  Consequently the boundary values are known:
  $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_0)=1$ for the noiseless channel, while the channel
  of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-depolarizing-channel} is entanglement-breaking
  precisely for $p\ge\tfrac{2}{3}$, where every output state is separable and
  $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)=0$.  The open interval is $0<p<\tfrac{2}{3}$.

  \item The only channel family with a known exact value is the erasure channel:
  Goodenough, Elkouss, and Wehner compute $E_{\mathrm{sq}}$ and the
  two-way-assisted capacities of the $d$-dimensional erasure channel in
  closed form, and for the depolarizing channel they improve the upper bound
  on $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ using convexity of $E_{\mathrm{sq}}$ in the
  channel and its link to the entanglement-assisted classical capacity
  \sourcecite{ref:sd-goodenough-elkouss-wehner}{GEW16}.

  \item Flagged extensions bound the quantum capacity of the depolarizing channel
  directly and surpass squashed-entanglement bounds in some regimes, but they
  do not evaluate $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$
  \sourcecite{ref:sd-kianvash-fanizza-giovannetti}{KFG22}.

  \item Riemannian optimization of channel extensions over the Stiefel manifold
  yields the strongest numerical upper bounds on the quantum capacity of the
  channel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-depolarizing-channel} at large $p$; the bounds
  do not meet the achievable rates, and the same work proves that
  amortization does not enhance the channel coherent information of any
  channel \sourcecite{ref:sd-zhu-mao-fang-wang}{ZMFW25}.

  \item The most recent dedicated work refines the flagged-extension technique
  into single-letter upper bounds on the quantum and private capacities of
  the depolarizing channel, with numerical coherent-information evaluation,
  and describes the channel as one whose capacity requires multi-letter
  superadditivity; it likewise leaves $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ uncomputed
  \sourcecite{ref:sd-nourozi}{Nour26}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The value of $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ is known only at the endpoints
$p=0$ and $p\ge\tfrac{2}{3}$ and is open on $0<p<\tfrac{2}{3}$.  Contributory
sub-questions: is the input maximum in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-squashed-entanglement} attained at the maximally entangled
state for every squashing map, or only within the restricted family used for
the known upper bound?  Are those squashing constructions optimal, or
improvable, on any subinterval of $p$?  Does $E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$
coincide with the symmetric coherent information
$\max\{0,1-H(\mathbf p)\}$ of the Pauli vector
$\mathbf p=\bigl(1-\tfrac{3p}{4},\tfrac{p}{4},\tfrac{p}{4},\tfrac{p}{4}\bigr)$,
or strictly exceed it somewhere?  A closed form would give the sharpest
computable single-letter upper bound on the two-way-assisted quantum and
private capacities of the depolarizing channel and on the two-way secret-key
rate of key distribution over depolarizing noise.  The sibling record
\texttt{01M20868D5Z2B0W2KKC7QGRGRR} asks for the private capacity
$P(\Lambda_p)$ of the same channel, a different quantity:
$E_{\mathrm{sq}}(\Lambda_p)$ upper-bounds the assisted capacity
$P_2(\Lambda_p)\ge P(\Lambda_p)$, and determining either value neither
settles nor is settled by the other.  The record
\texttt{01M1HME7809M71BG24CSMYKA8A} asks for the unassisted quantum capacity
$\mathcal Q(\Lambda_p)$ of the same channel family, which obeys
$\mathcal Q\le Q_2\le E_{\mathrm{sq}}$, and the record
\texttt{01M1HME780NTMHKFB95TSXKKFW} asks for the distillable entanglement of
Bell-diagonal states, which subsumes the two-way quantum capacity of this
channel through teleportation covariance, another rate that
$E_{\mathrm{sq}}$ upper-bounds.
