\section{Extensible causality and process purification}
\label{sec:op_a9d8fc135ea71ca1}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Is every finite-dimensional extensibly causal process matrix purifiable?
Call an $N$-laboratory process matrix $W$ extensibly causal if
$W\otimes\rho_R$ generates only causal correlations for every ancillary
input system $R$, every joint ancillary state $\rho_R$, and every choice of
local instruments.  Call $W$ purifiable if there exist auxiliary global past
and future systems, a fixed pure state on the auxiliary past, and a pure
process $S$ such that inserting the fixed state into $S$ and discarding the
auxiliary future yields $W$.  If $\mathrm{EC}_N$ and $\mathrm{Pur}_N$ denote
the corresponding classes, the question is whether
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{EC}_N\subseteq\mathrm{Pur}_N
  \label{eq:p35-ec-pur-inclusion}
\end{equation}
holds for every $N$ and every choice of finite local dimensions.  In
particular, does the inclusion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p35-ec-pur-inclusion} hold for
two laboratories?

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Ara\'ujo, Feix, Navascu\'es, and Brukner explicitly identify the possible
inclusion of extensibly causal processes in the purifiable class as open
\sourcecite{ref:p35-araujo}{AFN+17}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Extensible causality rules out activation of noncausal correlations
  by entangled ancillary inputs.  Ordinary causality is insufficient: there
  are causal processes that become noncausal after such an extension
  \sourcecite{ref:p35-oreshkov}{OG16}.

  \item Necessary algebraic conditions for purifiability exclude several
  process matrices that violate causal inequalities, but remain inconclusive
  for the noisy processes that motivated
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:p35-ec-pur-inclusion}
  \sourcecite{ref:p35-araujo}{AFN+17}.

  \item For two laboratories, every purifiable process is extensibly causal,
  so $\mathrm{Pur}_2\subseteq\mathrm{EC}_2$
  \sourcecite{ref:p35-yokojima}{YQS+21}.  This is the converse of the open
  implication in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p35-ec-pur-inclusion}; it neither proves the
  inclusion nor establishes equality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Ara\'ujo, Feix, Navascu\'es, and Brukner explicitly identify
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p35-ec-pur-inclusion} as an unresolved possibility and give the
noisy process built from their Eq.~(37) as a concrete test case.  The open
direction is extensible causality implying purifiability, not the proved
two-laboratory converse and not causal separability.
