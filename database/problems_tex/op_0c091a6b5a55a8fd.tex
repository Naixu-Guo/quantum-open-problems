\section{Asymptotically good quantum locally testable codes}
\label{sec:op_0c091a6b5a55a8fd}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Does there exist an infinite family of qubit CSS codes with linear rate and
linear distance whose check matrices have uniformly bounded row and column
weights and constant soundness?  Consider a family of qubit CSS codes
$[[n,k_n,d_n]]$ with $n\to\infty$.  Each code is specified by binary check
matrices $H_X\in\mathbb{F}_2^{m_X\times n}$ and
$H_Z\in\mathbb{F}_2^{m_Z\times n}$ with $m_X,m_Z>0$ and
$H_XH_Z^{\mathsf T}=0$.  Write $|\cdot|$ for Hamming weight.

Call the family \emph{good and locally testable} if there are constants
$r,\delta,s>0$ and $w\in\mathbb{N}$, independent of $n$, such that every code
in the family satisfies three conditions.  First, every row and every column
of $H_X$ and $H_Z$ has weight at most $w$.  Second, the rate and distance are
linear:
\begin{equation}
  k_n\ge rn,\qquad d_n\ge\delta n.
  \label{eq:0c09-good}
\end{equation}
Third, both check matrices have constant soundness:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{|H_\sigma e|}{m_\sigma}
  \ge
  s\,\frac{\min_{c\in\ker H_\sigma}|e-c|}{n}
  \qquad
  \text{for every }\sigma\in\{X,Z\}\text{ and }e\in\mathbb{F}_2^n.
  \label{eq:0c09-soundness}
\end{equation}
The question is whether some family satisfies the weight bound,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-good}, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-soundness} simultaneously.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Wills, Lin, and Hsieh describe the existence of an optimal quantum locally
testable code, with constant soundness, constant locality (maximum row or
column weight of $H_X$ and $H_Z$), and linear dimension and distance, as
currently unknown \sourcecite{ref:0c09-wlh}{WLH25}, arXiv v3, Section~1.1.
Cross, He, Natarajan, Szegedy, and Zhu call the construction of quantum
locally testable codes with good parameters an open problem
\sourcecite{ref:0c09-chnsz}{CHN+24}, Section~1, and Dinur, Lin, and Vidick
state that their almost-good construction falls short of good quantum locally
testable codes \sourcecite{ref:0c09-dlv}{DLV24}, arXiv v3, Section~1.4.  The
soundness condition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-soundness} is the CSS definition of
\sourcecite{ref:0c09-dlv}{DLV24}, Definition~2.6.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Equation~\eqref{eq:0c09-soundness} measures the distance to the tested
classical kernel $\ker H_\sigma$, not to the row space of the opposite check
matrix; a nontrivial logical operator has zero syndrome, so a definition using
the stabilizer row space would be violated by every code with $k_n\ge1$.  Up
to a factor of two in $s$, this CSS condition is equivalent to the general
soundness definition for quantum codes
\sourcecite{ref:0c09-dlv}{DLV24}, arXiv v3, Section~2.3, Lemma~2.5 and
Definition~2.6.

  \item Dinur, Lin, and Vidick construct an explicit family of binary CSS codes
with parity checks of weight $O(1)$ and
\begin{equation}
  k=\Omega(n),\qquad
  d=\Omega\!\left(\frac{n}{(\log n)^3}\right),\qquad
  s=\Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{(\log n)^3}\right)
  \label{eq:0c09-almost-good}
\end{equation}
\sourcecite{ref:0c09-dlv}{DLV24}, arXiv v3, Theorem~1.1 and Corollary~3.9.
The rate bound rests on the analysis of Section~4, which shifts the complex;
the arXiv revision of 4 September 2025 introduced it to correct an error in
the earlier rate argument, so it postdates the 2024 proceedings version.  The
distance and soundness in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-almost-good} miss Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-good} and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-soundness} by polylogarithmic factors.  The authors note
that their construction would give constant rate, relative distance, and
soundness with constant-weight checks if the graphs underlying the required
four-dimensional cubical complex were expanding up to a constant fraction of
the vertices; they do not know how to construct such a complex, and their
instantiation guarantees expansion only up to an inverse-polylogarithmic
fraction (remark after Corollary~3.7, and Section~1.6).

  \item The same paper states, with the proof omitted, that parity samplers raise
the soundness in Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-almost-good} to $\Omega(1)$ at check
weight $O((\log n)^3)$, and that distance amplification then yields CSS codes
with $k,d=\Omega(n)$, soundness $\Omega(1)$, and check weight
$O(\operatorname{poly}\log n)$ \sourcecite{ref:0c09-dlv}{DLV24}, arXiv v3,
Section~1 and footnote~1.  These codes do not have uniformly bounded
locality.

  \item Wills, Lin, and Hsieh give transformations between quantum locally
testable codes.  Soundness amplification keeps the number of qubits, the
dimension, and the distance, raises soundness $\rho$ to a constant, and
multiplies the maximum check weight and qubit degree by at most
$\operatorname{poly}(1/\rho)$.  Under further assumptions on the input code,
distance amplification produces linear distance with the same dimension and,
up to a constant factor, the same number of qubits, while locality and
soundness change by factors polynomial in the input locality and inverse
relative distance \sourcecite{ref:0c09-wlh}{WLH25}, arXiv v3, Theorems~1.2
and~1.3 (formally Lemmas~4.3 and~5.3).  Applied to inputs with vanishing
soundness or relative distance, these costs make the locality grow.  In
particular, soundness amplification of good quantum LDPC codes gives
asymptotically good quantum codes with constant soundness that are testable
but not locally testable (Section~1.2).

  \item Constant soundness alone is attainable with other tradeoffs.  Cross, He,
Natarajan, Szegedy, and Zhu obtain quantum LDPC codes with constant soundness,
constant rate, and distance $2$ (Lemma~1.1 and Corollary~3.2); their check
product gives constant soundness and rate with distance $\Theta(\log N)$ and
locality $O(\log N)$ (Theorem~1.2 and Table~2); and their modified distance
balancing gives constant soundness, $\Theta(\sqrt N)$ dimension and distance,
and constant \emph{average} locality, with maximum locality $\Theta(\sqrt N)$
(Theorem~1.3 and Table~2) \sourcecite{ref:0c09-chnsz}{CHN+24}.  Average
locality does not meet the uniform row and column bounds required here.

  \item Li, Li, and Liu construct almost-good quantum locally testable codes that
support nontrivial transversal logical multi-controlled-$Z$ gates, with
distance and soundness still polylogarithmically below optimal; their
revision of 2 July 2026 refers to good quantum locally testable codes only as
a prospective construction \sourcecite{ref:0c09-lll}{LLL26}, Theorem~1.1 and
Section~6.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Literature checked through 15 September 2026: no construction or
impossibility result was found for a family meeting the weight bound,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-good}, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:0c09-soundness} at once.  The
closest known results each give up part of the requirements: polylogarithmic
losses in distance and soundness at constant check weight, constant soundness
and linear parameters at polylogarithmic check weight, or constant soundness
and rate at bounded locality with constant distance.  A complete answer is
either such a family or a proof
that uniformly bounded locality, linear rate, linear distance, and constant
soundness are incompatible.

The cubical complexes of \sourcecite{ref:0c09-dlv}{DLV24} also underlie the
transversal-gate results of \sourcecite{ref:0c09-lll}{LLL26}; the analogous
question for a non-Clifford gate on good codes is
\href{https://qiqc-op.com/problem/op_15c6d5adf1796d86/}{good quantum LDPC codes
with a non-Clifford transversal CCZ gate}.
