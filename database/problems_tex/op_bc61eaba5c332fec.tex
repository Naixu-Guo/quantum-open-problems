\section{Optimal universal eight-copy concentration to a CCZ state}
\label{sec:op_bc61eaba5c332fec}

\paragraph{Problem.}
What is the optimal success probability for a fixed catalyst-free stabilizer protocol that converts eight copies of an arbitrary unknown pure qubit into an exact $|CCZ\rangle$ state?

A stabilizer operation is constructed from stabilizer ancillas, Clifford unitaries, Pauli measurements, classical randomness and feedforward, and discarding systems. Heralding is permitted where stated. It does not mean an arbitrary stabilizer-preserving channel, and no magic catalyst is free. These distinctions affect conversion theorems \sourcecite{ref:bc61-zurel26}{Zurel26}, \sourcecite{ref:bc61-fang26}{Fang26}.

Let $\mathcal U_8$ comprise fixed stabilizer protocols, chosen independently of the input, such that for every pure qubit state $\psi$, every accepted branch on $\psi^{\otimes8}$ outputs exactly $|CCZ\rangle$. Acceptance may be zero on stabilizer inputs. Define

\begin{equation}
|CCZ\rangle=2^{-3/2}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^3}(-1)^{x_1x_2x_3}|x\rangle.
\label{eq:bc61-1}
\end{equation}

Determine $p_8^*(\psi)=\sup_{\Lambda\in\mathcal U_8}p_\Lambda(\psi)$. In particular, is

\begin{equation}
p_8^*(\psi)=\frac23m_3(\psi),\qquad
m_3(\psi)=\frac{1-x^6-y^6-z^6}{2},
\label{eq:bc61-2}
\end{equation}

where $(x,y,z)$ is the pure-state Bloch vector? No magic catalysts are allowed.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:bc61-1}, \eqref{eq:bc61-2}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Rizzo and Leone establish

\begin{equation}
\frac23m_3(\psi)\leq p_8^*(\psi)\leq\frac76m_3(\psi)
\label{eq:bc61-3}
\end{equation}

and explicitly leave eight-copy optimality open \sourcecite{ref:bc61-rizzo26}{Rizzo26}. Equality with the lower bound is a candidate, not a published theorem or asserted consensus conjecture.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:bc61-3}.

  \item Status: open in the August 2026 preprint; no subsequent resolution identified.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

This finite-copy question offers a bounded input space, but “universal” is essential: optimizing a circuit for one known input is a different task. Its short history warrants more caution about community prominence than longer-established magic-state problems.
