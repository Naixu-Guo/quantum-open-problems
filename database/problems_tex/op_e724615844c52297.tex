\section{Smallest output dimension violating minimum output entropy additivity}
\label{sec:op_e724615844c52297}

\paragraph{Problem.}
What is the smallest output dimension in which the minimum output von Neumann
entropy of quantum channels fails to be additive?  For a density operator
$\sigma$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space write
$H(\sigma)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma\log_2\sigma)$ for its von Neumann
entropy, and for a quantum channel $\Phi$ define its minimum output entropy by
\begin{equation}
  S_{\min}(\Phi):=\min_{\rho\in\mathcal D(A)}H\bigl(\Phi(\rho)\bigr),
  \label{eq:dmin-minimum-output-entropy}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal D(A)$ is the set of density operators on the input space $A$
of $\Phi$.  A product input is always available in $\Phi\otimes\Psi$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-minimum-output-entropy} gives
$S_{\min}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)\leq S_{\min}(\Phi)+S_{\min}(\Psi)$ for any two
channels; the phenomenon at issue is the strict inequality
\begin{equation}
  S_{\min}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)<S_{\min}(\Phi)+S_{\min}(\Psi),
  \label{eq:dmin-violation}
\end{equation}
and a pair $(\Phi,\Psi)$ of channels satisfying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-violation} is called a violation.  Define the
output-dimension threshold
\begin{equation}
  d_{\min}:=\min\bigl\{d\in\mathbb N:\ \text{some violation }(\Phi,\Psi)
  \text{ has both output spaces of dimension at most }d\bigr\},
  \label{eq:dmin-threshold}
\end{equation}
with input and environment dimensions arbitrary.  Determine the exact value of
$d_{\min}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold}.  The companion constructive target, also open at every output dimension, is a
practically computable, explicitly presented pair of finite-dimensional
channels with a certified instance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-violation}:
deterministic asymptotic algorithms now output violating pairs once their
size parameter is sufficiently large, but none supplies a practically
computable finite-dimensional instance.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Hastings settled the qualitative existence of violations of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-violation} probabilistically
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-hastings}{Has09}.  The threshold formulation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold} is derived from the quantitative dimension
analysis of Belinschi, Collins, and Nechita
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-belinschi}{BCN16} together with the recorded constructive status at $p=1$ --- where
deterministic asymptotic algorithms exist but no practically computable
finite-dimensional realization is known \sourcecite{ref:dmin-leung}{LLW26},
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-lovwu}{LovWu26}, \sourcecite{ref:dmin-zzcw}{ZZCW26};
no single source states it as a numbered open problem.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hastings disproved additivity of the minimum output von Neumann entropy with
finite-dimensional random channels, so the set in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold} is nonempty; the construction is probabilistic
and yields no explicit pair \sourcecite{ref:dmin-hastings}{Has09}, as are its
later simplifications \sourcecite{ref:dmin-brandao-horodecki}{BH10}.

  \item Belinschi, Collins, and Nechita computed the exact limiting minimum output
entropy of a random channel in the conjugate-pair ensemble, exhibited
violations of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-violation} with size approaching one bit,
and proved that their Bell-state criterion detects a violation at output
dimension $183$ while failing almost surely up to dimension $182$ within
their ensemble; the bound $d_{\min}\leq183$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold}
follows, but the threshold is ensemble-specific \sourcecite{ref:dmin-belinschi}{BCN16}.

  \item Leung, Lovitz, and Wu proved nonadditivity of the minimum output R\'enyi
entropy for $p\in[0,1/4)\cup(3/4,\infty]$
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-leung}{LLW26}.  At $p=1$ all their arguments remain
probabilistic; within their locally normalized projection ensemble,
high-precision numerical optimization evaluates the Bell-state criterion as
detecting a violation already at output dimension $182$, against $183$ for
the ensemble criterion of \sourcecite{ref:dmin-belinschi}{BCN16}, a
threshold they state is ensemble- and witness-specific and not universal
over all channels.  Deterministic asymptotic algorithms followed: Lovitz
and Wu replace Haar randomness by permutations and derandomize via the
O'Donnell--Wu construction, polynomial-time for fixed channel parameters
and accuracy but with size estimates beyond practical reach
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-lovwu}{LovWu26}, and Zhen, Zhu, Chen, and Wang,
Theorem~2.1, deterministically output near-free permutation representations
with a certified entropy gap for all sufficiently large target sizes
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-zzcw}{ZZCW26}; neither supplies a practically computed
instance.

  \item Counterexamples away from the von Neumann order $p=1$ split by construction
type: Hayden and Winter proved nonadditivity for every $p>1$ using random
channels \sourcecite{ref:dmin-hayden-winter}{HW08}, while the deterministic
constructions are due to Grudka, Horodecki, and Pankowski for every $p>2$
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-grudka}{GHP10} and to Derksen and Lovitz for every
$p>1$, from subspaces with high geometric measure of entanglement
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-derksen-lovitz}{DL26}.  None of these produces a
violation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-violation} at the von Neumann order $p=1$.

  \item Krohn-Grimberghe certified, with exact rational witnesses, a strict violation
of minimum output R\'enyi-entropy additivity for all $0<p\leq1/22$ for the
explicit four-to-three channel pair of Cubitt et al.
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-krohn}{KG26}, \sourcecite{ref:dmin-cubitt}{CHLMW08}:
explicit small-dimension violations exist at small R\'enyi orders, sharpening
the contrast with the open $p=1$ case.

  \item Collins and Youn violated additivity of the regularized minimum output
entropy through a non-random construction on an infinite-dimensional
commuting-operator system and noted that it is not clear the approach works in
finite dimensions, leaving Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold} untouched
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-collins-youn}{CY22}.

  \item King proved that unital qubit channels are additive with an arbitrary partner
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-king}{Kin02}, so a violation in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold} at $d=2$ would require both channels to be
non-unital; beyond the trivial $d_{\min}\geq2$, reflecting that
one-dimensional outputs are additive with every partner, no lower bound is
known.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The unresolved gap is the range $2\leq d_{\min}\leq183$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-threshold}: the rigorous upper bound is the
ensemble-specific analysis of \sourcecite{ref:dmin-belinschi}{BCN16}, whose
Bell-state criterion detects a violation at output dimension $183$ and fails
almost surely through $182$ within that ensemble, while the threshold $182$
of \sourcecite{ref:dmin-leung}{LLW26} is the numerical evaluation, by
high-precision optimization, of the corresponding criterion in a different,
locally normalized ensemble --- numerical evidence that a violation may
already exist at output dimension $182$, not a certified bound, so the
certified upper endpoint remains $183$; the lower bound is the trivial
additivity of one-dimensional outputs, and the strongest structural
constraint is that a $d=2$ violation needs two non-unital channels
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-king}{Kin02}.  No practically computable
finite-dimensional pair realizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmin-violation} at $p=1$ is
known in any dimension: existence is probabilistic
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-hastings}{Has09}, \sourcecite{ref:dmin-leung}{LLW26},
and the deterministic algorithms of \sourcecite{ref:dmin-lovwu}{LovWu26} and
\sourcecite{ref:dmin-zzcw}{ZZCW26} reach violations only asymptotically,
without a practical instance; that constructive target is archived in the
\href{https://qiqc-op.com/problem/op_25e23d6e92ebce9d/}{closed-form nonadditivity record}.  The sibling extremal question of the
maximal violation size
$S_{\min}(\Phi)+S_{\min}(\Psi)-S_{\min}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)$ at fixed output
dimension is not archived here.  The \href{https://qiqc-op.com/problem/op_c0b1045a614d2353/}{delayed-onset additivity-violation problem} poses the orthogonal onset question for a single channel and its
tensor powers, so neither record subsumes the other.
