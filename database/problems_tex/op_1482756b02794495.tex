\section{Amortization collapse for superchannel divergences}
\label{sec:op_1482756b02794495}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Does amortization collapse for the geometric R\'enyi divergence of
arbitrary finite-dimensional quantum superchannels?
For compatible states, let
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
    &:=\inf\{\lambda:\rho\leq2^\lambda\sigma\},\\
  \widehat D_\alpha(\rho\|\sigma)
    &:=\frac1{\alpha-1}\log_2\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
      \sigma\bigl(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\bigr)^\alpha
      \right],\qquad 1<\alpha\leq2,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:p68-state-divergences}
\end{equation}
with the standard support conventions.  For either divergence
$\mathbf D\in\{D_{\max},\widehat D_\alpha\}$, define its channel extension
and channel-amortized extension by
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathbf D_{\rm ch}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M)
    &:=\sup_{\rho_{RA}}
       \mathbf D(\mathcal N(\rho)\|\mathcal M(\rho)),\\
  \mathbf D_{\rm ch}^{A}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M)
    &:=\sup_{\rho_{RA},\sigma_{RA}}
       \{\mathbf D(\mathcal N(\rho)\|\mathcal M(\sigma))
         -\mathbf D(\rho\|\sigma)\},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:p68-channel-amortization}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-channel-amortization}, identity maps on $R$ are implicit,
and the optimizations allow an arbitrary reference of sufficient finite
dimension.  For superchannels
$\Theta_1,\Theta_2$, set
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathbf D_{\rm sc}(\Theta_1\|\Theta_2)
    &:=\sup_{\mathcal N}
      \mathbf D_{\rm ch}(\Theta_1(\mathcal N)\|\Theta_2(\mathcal N)),\\
  \mathbf D_{\rm sc}^{A}(\Theta_1\|\Theta_2)
    &:=\sup_{\mathcal N,\mathcal M}
      \{\mathbf D_{\rm ch}^{A}
          (\Theta_1(\mathcal N)\|\Theta_2(\mathcal M))
        -\mathbf D_{\rm ch}^{A}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M)\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:p68-superchannel-amortization}
\end{equation}
Is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf D_{\rm sc}^{A}(\Theta_1\|\Theta_2)
  =\mathbf D_{\rm sc}(\Theta_1\|\Theta_2)
  \label{eq:p68-superchannel-collapse}
\end{equation}
for $\mathbf D=\widehat D_\alpha$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-state-divergences},
$1<\alpha\leq2$, and all superchannel pairs? The definitions also include $D_{\max}$ to state its settled
subcase below. The amortized suprema use pairs with finite subtracted
divergence; other infinite values have the standard support convention.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Hirche's Remark 5.4, after Eqs.~(75)--(77), leaves amortization collapse
open in general superchannel discrimination settings
\sourcecite{ref:p68-hirche}{Hir23}. For the nested definition in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-superchannel-amortization}, the max-relative-entropy
case follows directly from CP order, as shown below; the remaining target
here is the geometric R\'enyi case.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item For point-to-point channels, amortization of the max-relative entropy
  collapses:
  \begin{equation}
    D_{\max,{\rm ch}}^{A}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M)
    =D_{\max,{\rm ch}}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M).
    \label{eq:p68-channel-max-collapse}
  \end{equation}
  The proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-channel-max-collapse} uses the CP-order
  structure of channel max-relative entropy
  \sourcecite{ref:p68-wilde-berta-hirche-kaur}{WBHK20}.

  \item Fang and Fawzi proved the analogous point-to-point channel collapse
  \begin{equation}
    \widehat D_{\alpha,{\rm ch}}^{A}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M)
    =\widehat D_{\alpha,{\rm ch}}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M),
    \qquad 1<\alpha\leq2,
    \label{eq:p68-channel-geometric-collapse}
  \end{equation}
  from a chain rule for the geometric R\'enyi divergence
  \sourcecite{ref:p68-fang-fawzi}{FF21}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:p68-channel-geometric-collapse} concerns point-to-point
channels; it does not settle the superchannel claim.

  \item For the nested superchannel definition, the max-relative-entropy
subcase also collapses. Indeed, let
$t=2^{D_{\max,\mathrm{ch}}(\mathcal N\|\mathcal M)}$ and
$s=2^{D_{\max,\mathrm{sc}}(\Theta_1\|\Theta_2)}$ be finite.
Channel max-relative entropy is characterized by CP order, and physical
superchannels preserve that order. Thus
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal N\leq_{\mathrm{CP}}t\mathcal M
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Theta_1(\mathcal N)\leq_{\mathrm{CP}}t\Theta_1(\mathcal M)
    \leq_{\mathrm{CP}}ts\Theta_2(\mathcal M),
  \label{eq:p68-nested-max-order}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal A\leq_{\mathrm{CP}}\mathcal B$ means
$\mathcal B-\mathcal A$ is completely positive.
Taking logarithms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-nested-max-order} and using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-channel-max-collapse} bounds each term of the amortized
supremum by $\log_2s$. Choosing $\mathcal N=\mathcal M$ gives the reverse
inequality, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-superchannel-collapse} for
$\mathbf D=D_{\max}$. If the unamortized divergence is infinite, the same
reverse inequality already proves equality in the extended sense.
This is a direct consequence of the cited channel CP-order characterization
\sourcecite{ref:p68-wilde-berta-hirche-kaur}{WBHK20}; it does not prove
collapse of Hirche's larger fully amortized quantities
\sourcecite{ref:p68-hirche}{Hir23}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The remaining displayed question is geometric R\'enyi amortization
collapse for nested-adaptive superchannel discrimination. The
max-relative-entropy subcase is settled by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p68-nested-max-order}. Whether the larger fully amortized
divergences controlling braided and fully general strategies collapse is a
further, stronger question.
