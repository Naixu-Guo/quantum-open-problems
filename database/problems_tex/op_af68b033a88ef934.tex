\section{Permanent-of-Gaussians Conjecture}
\label{sec:op_af68b033a88ef934}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Is the following estimation task $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard under randomized
polynomial-time Turing reductions? Given $X\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$ with
independent entries $X_{ij}=(a_{ij}+ib_{ij})/\sqrt{2}$, where all
$a_{ij},b_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independently, and
$\epsilon,\delta\in(0,1)$, output $z(X)\in\mathbb{C}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Per}(X):=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j,\sigma(j)},
  \qquad
  \Pr_X\!\left[|z(X)-\operatorname{Per}(X)|
    \leq\epsilon|\operatorname{Per}(X)|\right]\geq1-\delta.
  \label{eq:pgc-estimation}
\end{equation}
Here $S_n$ is the set of permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
The guarantee in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pgc-estimation} is required for arbitrary
$\epsilon,\delta$, with running time measured in $n,1/\epsilon,1/\delta$.

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

Aaronson and Arkhipov's Permanent-of-Gaussians Conjecture:
Conjecture 5 and the preceding definition of $\mathrm{GPE}_{\times}$
in arXiv:1011.3245, Section 1.2.3 (2010 preprint; published 2013)
\sourcecite{ref:pgc-aa}{AA13}.
The original task approximates the complex permanent itself with relative error.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item 2010--2013: Aaronson--Arkhipov prove exact Gaussian-permanent
$\#\mathrm{P}$-hardness at success fraction
$3/4+1/\operatorname{poly}(n)$, and hardness for estimating
$|\operatorname{Per}(X)|^2$ with exponentially small additive error
(arXiv Theorems 60 and 62) \sourcecite{ref:pgc-aa}{AA13}.

  \item 2021: Bouland--Fefferman--Landau--Liu obtain additive tolerance
$e^{-4n\log n-O(n)}$ for $|\operatorname{Per}(X)|^2$, with any constant
failure probability below $1/4$, using $\mathrm{BPP}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ reductions
(proof of Corollary 1) \sourcecite{ref:pgc-bfll}{BFLL22}.

  \item 2023 (unpublished): Krovi's tighter total-variation estimate improves
this tolerance to $e^{-2n\log n-O(n)}$, as reported and explained by
Bouland et al. (Section 1.2.1) \sourcecite{ref:pgc-bdfh}{BDFH25}.

  \item 2025: Bouland et al.'s dilution method proves $\#\mathrm{P}$-hardness at
additive error $n!e^{-O(n^\gamma)}$ for $|\operatorname{Per}(X)|^2$,
for every fixed $\gamma>0$, with constant success probability, assuming
permanent anticoncentration (Theorem 1, rescaled;
$\mathrm{BPP}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ reduction) \sourcecite{ref:pgc-bdfh}{BDFH25}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

The original conjecture remains open. The squared-permanent error
target is $\epsilon n!$; its equivalence to the relative-error task uses
permanent anticoncentration (arXiv Theorem 7) \sourcecite{ref:pgc-aa}{AA13}.
Koehler--Leung's July 2026 preprint proves this auxiliary conjecture
(Theorem 1.1) \sourcecite{ref:pgc-kl}{KL26}.
The hardness tolerance $n!e^{-O(n^\gamma)}$ still falls short of
$n!/\operatorname{poly}(n)$ \sourcecite{ref:pgc-bdfh}{BDFH25}.
