\section{Zero two-way quantum capacity of endpoint Holevo--Werner channels}
\label{sec:op_fc663252f5664f40}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Is the two-way assisted quantum capacity of the Holevo--Werner channel
$\mathcal W_d$ zero for every integer $d\geq3$?  For $d\geq3$, define
$\mathcal W_d:\mathcal L(\mathbb C^d)\to\mathcal L(\mathbb C^d)$ by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal W_d(X):=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(X)I_d-\frac12X^{\mathsf T}}{d-\frac12},
  \label{eq:fc66-channel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathsf T$ denotes the transpose in the computational basis.  Let $S_d$
be the swap operator on $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$, and let
$\phi_d:=\lvert\phi_d\rangle\!\langle\phi_d\rvert$ with
$\lvert\phi_d\rangle:=d^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\lvert jj\rangle$.  The channel in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-channel} has the normalized Choi state
\begin{equation}
  \rho_d:=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\mathcal W_d)(\phi_d)
  =\frac{I_{d^2}-\frac12S_d}{d^2-\frac d2}.
  \label{eq:fc66-choi-state}
\end{equation}
Let $Q_{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal W_d)$ be the supremum of the rates, in
qubits per channel use, that can be transmitted with vanishing error by
protocols that interleave uses of $\mathcal W_d$ with adaptive local
operations and unlimited two-way classical communication, starting without
shared entanglement.  The question is whether
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal W_d)=0
  \qquad\text{for every integer }d\geq3 .
  \label{eq:fc66-question}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Status}

Unsolved

\subsection*{Source}

The Choi states in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-choi-state} belong to the Werner family
in which DiVincenzo, Shor, Smolin, Terhal, and Thapliyal conjecture the
existence of undistillable states with non-positive partial transpose
(Section~II) \sourcecite{ref:fc66-divincenzo-et-al}{DSS+00}.  Bharti,
Gajjala, and Haug state undistillability of this endpoint for all copy
numbers as the remaining unrestricted many-copy conjecture (Eq.~(4))
\sourcecite{ref:fc66-bharti-et-al}{BGH26}.  The channel formulation uses the
teleportation covariance of Holevo--Werner channels
\sourcecite{ref:fc66-cope-goodenough-pirandola}{CGP18}.

\subsection*{Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item In the Werner-state convention
  $\rho_{d,\alpha}:=(I_{d^2}+\alpha S_d)/(d^2+\alpha d)$ with
  $-1\leq\alpha\leq1$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-choi-state} is
  $\rho_d=\rho_{d,-1/2}$.  Partial transposition on the second factor gives
  \begin{equation}
    \rho_d^{T_B}=\frac{I_{d^2}-\frac d2\,\phi_d}{d^2-\frac d2},
    \label{eq:fc66-partial-transpose}
  \end{equation}
  whose eigenvalue on $\lvert\phi_d\rangle$ is negative for every $d\geq3$, so
  zero capacity cannot follow from a PPT Choi state.  Up to a positive factor,
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-partial-transpose} is the operator
  $\lambda I-(\lambda+1)\lvert\Phi_0\rangle\!\langle\Phi_0\rvert$ with
  $\lambda=2/(d-2)$ of DiVincenzo, Shor, Smolin, Terhal, and Thapliyal, the
  transition point between one-copy distillable Werner states and Werner
  states with nonnegative expectation on all Schmidt-rank-two vectors
  (Section~III~A); following Horodecki et al., they conjecture that some NPT
  Werner states are undistillable
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-divincenzo-et-al}{DSS+00}.  The partial transpose and
  this identification were checked directly for this entry.

  \item Cope, Goodenough, and Pirandola define Holevo--Werner channels as the
  channels whose Choi states are Werner states and show that they are
  teleportation covariant; in the present parameterization, their Eq.~(8)
  reads
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal W_d(UXU^\dagger)=\overline U\,\mathcal W_d(X)\,U^{\mathsf T}
    \qquad(U\in\mathrm U(d)).
    \label{eq:fc66-covariance}
  \end{equation}
  The channel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-channel} is their Eq.~(6) with expectation
  parameter $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_dS_d)=(2-d)/(2d-1)$, and their
  $\alpha$-representation uses the opposite sign of $\alpha$
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-cope-goodenough-pirandola}{CGP18}.  By
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-covariance}, the channel is Choi-stretchable: each use
  can be simulated by an LOCC operation acting on one copy of $\rho_d$
  (Proposition~2) \sourcecite{ref:fc66-plob}{PLOB17}.

  \item Teleportation stretching reduces an adaptive protocol with $n$ uses of
  $\mathcal W_d$ to an LOCC operation on $\rho_d^{\otimes n}$, while sending
  halves of $\phi_d$ through the channel gives the reverse inequality; hence
  \begin{equation}
    Q_{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal W_d)=D_{\leftrightarrow}(\rho_d),
    \label{eq:fc66-capacity-distillation}
  \end{equation}
  where $D_{\leftrightarrow}$ is the two-way LOCC distillable entanglement.
  Cope, Goodenough, and Pirandola state this identity for finite-dimensional
  teleportation-covariant channels through a reduction to entanglement
  distillation (Appendix~A)
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-cope-goodenough-pirandola}{CGP18}; see also
  Supplementary Note~10 of \sourcecite{ref:fc66-plob}{PLOB17}.  By the
  finite-copy distillability criterion (Lemmas~1 and~2)
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-divincenzo-et-al}{DSS+00},
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-question} is equivalent to requiring, for every
  $d\geq3$,
  \begin{equation}
    \langle\psi\rvert\bigl(I_{d^2}-\tfrac d2\,\phi_d\bigr)^{\otimes k}
    \lvert\psi\rangle\geq0
    \quad\text{for all }k\geq1\text{ and all }\lvert\psi\rangle\text{ with }
    \operatorname{SR}_{R^k:B^k}(\lvert\psi\rangle)\leq2,
    \label{eq:fc66-criterion}
  \end{equation}
  where $\operatorname{SR}_{R^k:B^k}$ is the Schmidt rank between the $k$
  reference systems and the $k$ output systems.

  \item Four preprints posted in July 2026 prove the two-copy threshold: for
  every $d\geq2$ and $-1\leq\alpha\leq1$, the state $\rho_{d,\alpha}$ is
  two-copy undistillable if and only if $\alpha\geq-1/2$ (Theorem~1.1 of
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-fu-gao-park}{FGP26}, Theorem~5 of
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-song-chen}{SC26}, Corollary~C of
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-fraser-et-al}{FHPV26}, and Corollary~1.2 of
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-bharti-et-al}{BGH26}).  This establishes
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-criterion} for $k\leq2$ in every dimension, but not for
  $k\geq3$.

  \item Bharti, Gajjala, and Haug give equivalent forms of the all-copy endpoint
  condition, including 2-positivity of $\Phi_d^{\otimes k}$ for every $k$,
  where $\Phi_d(X):=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I_d-\frac12X$.  They explain why the
  two-copy proof does not induct: a partial trace can increase rank, and
  tensor products need not preserve 2-positivity.  They prove the endpoint
  inequality for tensor-factorized witnesses whose rank-two factor is
  supported on at most two copies, and they construct constants $\gamma_k>0$,
  with $\gamma_3=1/6$, such that $\rho_{d,\alpha}$ is $k$-copy undistillable
  for $\alpha\geq-\gamma_k$ in every dimension.  For $k\geq3$, neither result
  reaches $\alpha=-1/2$ \sourcecite{ref:fc66-bharti-et-al}{BGH26}.

  \item For three copies, write a test vector as
  $\lvert\psi\rangle=\operatorname{vec}C$, so that its Schmidt rank is
  $\operatorname{rank}C$.  Wu and Zou prove the three-copy endpoint inequality
  for every positive semidefinite and every normal $C$ of rank at most two
  (Theorem~1.3 and Corollary~6.1), and for nonnormal $C$ under local support
  conditions (Theorems~7.2 and~7.3).  General nonnormal rank-two witnesses
  remain open, so three-copy undistillability of $\rho_d$ is not established
  \sourcecite{ref:fc66-wu-zou}{WZ26}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Comment}

Literature checked through 15 September 2026.  No proof or disproof of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-criterion} for any $k\geq3$ and $d\geq3$ was located; the
remaining gap is undistillability of $\rho_{d,-1/2}$ at three or more copies.
The two-copy results and the three-copy sector results are preprints.  A proof
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-question} for one fixed $d\geq3$ would already exhibit
NPT bound entanglement, answering the existence question of the
\href{https://qiqc-op.com/problem/op_74c4ffb5b042baf2/}{NPT bound entanglement problem}
affirmatively; the present record asks for more, namely undistillability of
this specific endpoint state in every dimension.  Restricting Schmidt-rank-two
test vectors to $\mathbb C^{d'}\otimes\mathbb C^{d'}$ in each copy shows that
$k$-copy distillability of $\rho_{d',-1/2}$ implies that of $\rho_{d,-1/2}$
for every $d\geq d'$; hence Eq.~\eqref{eq:fc66-question} holds for every
$d\geq3$ as soon as it holds for infinitely many $d$.  Mixing with separable
Werner states likewise transfers undistillability from $\alpha=-1/2$ to every
$-1/2\leq\alpha<-1/d$.  These two reductions are deductions made for this
entry.
