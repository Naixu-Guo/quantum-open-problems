Nice error bases

The source asked whether every nice error basis is of shift and multiply type. Klappenecker and Roetteler answered negatively by constructing an abstract error group with a non-monomial irreducible representation.

The accepted status decision records this problem as solved, so it no longer appears in the active explorer. The statement, resolution evidence, and bibliography stay at this URL.

Why it matters

Orthogonal bases of unitaries are exactly what is needed to build teleportation and dense-coding schemes and underlie stabilizer code theory, and the question asked whether the algebraic notion of a nice error basis coincides with the combinatorial shift-and-multiply notion built from Latin squares and complex Hadamard matrices.

Decide the following question: is every nice error basis of shift and multiply type? Equivalently, given an orthogonal basis \(\{U_1, \ldots, U_{d^2}\}\) of unitaries on \(\mathbf{C}^d\) such that \(\mathrm{tr}(U_i^{*} U_j) = d\,\delta_{ij}\) and \(U_i U_j = \omega_{ij} U_k\) for some phases \(\omega_{ij}\), does there exist a unitary change of basis of \(\mathbf{C}^d\) after which every \(U_i\) is a product of a permutation matrix and a diagonal unitary?

Verified Refutes nice-error-bases-shift-and-multiply

Klappenecker and Roetteler exhibit a non-monomial nice error basis

Klappenecker and Roetteler build, from representation-theoretic results of Ferguson and Isaacs on groups of central type, an abstract error group with cyclic centre possessing a non-monomial irreducible representation. The corresponding nice error basis is therefore not monomial and in particular not of shift and multiply type, so the archived universal claim is false.

  1. 2003
    Preprint Exact counterexample arXiv:quant-ph/0301078 ↗ Character-theoretic construction of a non-monomial nice error basis
Editorial narrative Status and known progress as written by the editors

Solved (in the negative). A. Klappenecker and M. Roetteler answered the question by exhibiting a nice error basis that is not of shift and multiply type, in the paper On the Monomiality of Nice Error Bases [quant-ph/0301078] (2003). The source records the "last progress" date as 28 May 2003 and lists A. Klappenecker and M. Roetteler as the solvers.

The construction is character-theoretic: every nice error basis of shift and multiply type is monomial (each basis unitary has exactly one non-zero matrix entry per row and column). An abstract error group — the central extension of the index group generated by a nice error basis — is a group of central type with cyclic centre. Building on representation-theoretic results of Ferguson and Isaacs for groups of central type, Klappenecker and Roetteler constructed an abstract error group possessing a non-monomial irreducible representation; the corresponding nice error basis is therefore not monomial and in particular not of shift and multiply type.

Earlier related results that frame the question:

  • E. Knill [quant-ph/9608049] introduced nice error bases and showed their role in error-correcting codes beyond the stabilizer formalism.
  • A. Klappenecker and M. Roetteler [quant-ph/0010082], Beyond Stabilizer Codes I: Nice Error Bases (2000; published in IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory 48, 2392 (2002)), classified nice error bases of small order and developed the systematic theory underlying both the shift-and-multiply construction and the eventual counterexample.
  • R. F. Werner [quant-ph/0003070] tied orthogonal bases of unitaries to the construction of teleportation and dense-coding schemes.

Thus the headline question — "is every nice error basis of shift and multiply type?" — is resolved negatively, and the classification of nice error bases of small order is given in [quant-ph/0010082]; the broader classification of all abstract error groups (and of all nice error bases up to equivalence) in arbitrary dimension is still active.
