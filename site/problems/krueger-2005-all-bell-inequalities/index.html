Classify all Bell inequalities

Facet lists are complete only for small scenarios or restricted correlation spaces. No closed-form classification exists for arbitrary parties, settings and outcomes.

On this page Statement What remains Progress Interfaces Cite

Why it matters

Facet inequalities give the complete linear tests for classical locality in a fixed experiment. A general classification would organize Bell tests and identify which constraints can show quantum violations.

Find all linear inequalities characterising the existence of joint probability distributions for all variables in a correlation experiment with parameters \((N,M,K)\). Equivalently, characterize the local polytope generated by the \(K^{NM}\) deterministic vertices inside the affine subspace of its \((MK)^N\) raw probability coordinates.

The problem is posed not only in the general \((N,M,K)\) form, but with several explicit sub-tasks:

  • find complete solutions for further small values of \((N,M,K)\);
  • find efficient ways of generating new inequalities, i.e., inequalities that cannot be written as convex combinations of lower-order ones;
  • find infinite families of new inequalities, possibly characterised by symmetries;
  • restrict to "full correlation functions", i.e., disregard constraints on marginal distributions;
  • specialise to correlation inequalities with \(K=2\), conveniently written in terms of expectations \(\mathsf{E}(A_1 A_2 \cdots A_N)\) with \(A_i \in \{+1,-1\}\) (or \(-1 \le A_i \le 1\));
  • decide by what margin the inequalities can be violated by quantum states, in general and under additional constraints (fixed Hilbert-space dimension, symmetry, positive partial transposes).
What remains

Classify the facets of the local correlation polytope for general numbers of parties, settings and outcomes.
