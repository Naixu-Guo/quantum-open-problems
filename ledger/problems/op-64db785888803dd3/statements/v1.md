Can shared randomness and one classical bit exactly simulate every pair of local projective measurements on every pure entangled two-qubit state? In Schmidt form the state is

$$
\lvert\psi_p\rangle
 =\sqrt p\,\lvert00\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}\,\lvert11\rangle,
 \qquad \frac12<p<1.
\tag{1}
$$

For arbitrary Bloch vectors $\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in S^2$ and outcomes $a,b\in\{0,1\}$, the target distribution associated with Eq. (1) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
 P_p(a,b\mid\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
 \lvert\psi_p\rangle\!\langle\psi_p\rvert
 \bigl(M_{a\mid\mathbf x}\otimes M_{b\mid\mathbf y}\bigr)
 \right],\\
 M_{a\mid\mathbf x}
 &=\frac{I+(-1)^a\mathbf x\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2},
 \qquad
 M_{b\mid\mathbf y}
 =\frac{I+(-1)^b\mathbf y\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

The protocol may use unlimited shared randomness; after receiving $\mathbf x$, Alice sends Bob one bit, and their local outputs must reproduce Eq. (2) for every pair of measurement directions. If no such universal protocol exists, find a finite-setting linear inequality satisfied by all one-bit protocols and violated by one of these target distributions.
