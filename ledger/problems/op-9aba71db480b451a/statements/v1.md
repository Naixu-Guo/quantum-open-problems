Does a polynomial-length random walk generated by ordinary two-qubit gates form a pseudorandom-unitary ensemble?

A function $\varepsilon(n)$ is negligible if, for every constant $a>0$, eventually $\varepsilon(n)<n^{-a}$. “QPT” denotes a quantum polynomial-time algorithm.

For an ensemble $\mathcal U_n$ of efficiently implementable $n$-qubit unitaries, the PRU requirement used here is

$$
\left|\Pr_{U\sim\mathcal U_n}[\mathcal D^U(1^n)=1]
-\Pr_{V\sim\operatorname{Haar}(2^n)}[\mathcal D^V(1^n)=1]\right|
\leq \operatorname{negl}(n)
\tag{1}
$$

for every QPT distinguisher $\mathcal D$. A single unitary is sampled and reused throughout the experiment. Queries may be adaptive and use entangled inputs and auxiliary registers, but access to $U^\dagger$, $U^T$, $U^*$, or a controlled-$U$ oracle is not automatically granted. The sampled key or circuit description is hidden. This is the forward-query notion originating in [Ji18](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/544.pdf), Sections 6.1.

A fixed efficient generator must work against every polynomial-time adversary. A construction whose size is retuned to the adversary’s chosen polynomial query bound does not establish that requirement. This distinction between cryptographic pseudorandomness and bounded-order designs is already emphasized in [Ji18](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/544.pdf), Introduction.

Conjecture. A polynomial number of ordinary random two-qubit gates suffices to generate a PRU ensemble.

Fix a finite universal two-qubit gate set $\mathcal G\subset SU(4)$. Independently at each step, choose a random qubit pair and a random gate from $\mathcal G$. Let $U_{n,m}$ be the resulting $n$-qubit circuit unitary. Are there constants $c>0$ and $C_{\mathcal G}>0$ such that

$$
\mathcal U_n
=\left\{U_{n,m(n)}:
 m(n)=\left\lceil C_{\mathcal G}n^c\right\rceil\right\}
\tag{2}
$$

is a PRU ensemble? This is the natural-random-circuit conjecture formulated in [Bostanci25](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.TQC.2025.9), Conjecture 1.1, restricted to a polynomial-length sampling procedure.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2).
