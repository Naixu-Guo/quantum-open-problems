Does the four-party Zhang–Yeung inequality hold for the von Neumann entropies of every finite-dimensional quantum state?

For arbitrary jointly distributed finite-valued random variables $A,B,C,D$, the Zhang–Yeung inequality is

$$
2I(C:D)\le I(A:B)+I(A:CD)+3I(C:D\mid A)+I(C:D\mid B).
\tag{1}
$$

Zhang and Yeung proved it in 1998. It is not a consequence of the basic Shannon inequalities. Dougherty, Freiling, and Zeger provide a subsequent proof and a systematic treatment of four-variable non-Shannon inequalities. [Zhang98](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.681320)[DFZ11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3602)

The question is whether the same inequality constrains quantum entropies. Let $\rho_{ABCD}$ be an arbitrary density operator on four finite-dimensional systems. Define

$$
S(R)=-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_R\log_2\rho_R,
\tag{2}
$$

$$
I(A:B)=S(A)+S(B)-S(AB),
\tag{3}
$$

$$
I(A:B\mid C)=S(AC)+S(BC)-S(C)-S(ABC).
\tag{4}
$$

Question: Does the displayed inequality, with these quantum mutual informations, hold for every $\rho_{ABCD}$?

There are no independence, separability, stabilizer, or holographic assumptions. Bao, Cao, Walter, and Wang explicitly discuss this inequality as a candidate constraint on general quantum entropies in Section 4.2. [Bao15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05650v2)

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2), (3), (4).
