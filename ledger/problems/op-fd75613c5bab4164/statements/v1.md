Do there exist finite-dimensional quantum channels that are less noisy in Watanabe’s regularized sense but are not degradable? Let $V_{A\to BE}$ be a Stinespring isometry defining a channel and a complementary channel by

$$
\mathcal N_{A\to B}(X)
 :=\operatorname{Tr}_E[VXV^\dagger],
 \qquad
 \mathcal N^c_{A\to E}(X)
 :=\operatorname{Tr}_B[VXV^\dagger].
\tag{1}
$$

The channel in Eq. (1) is degradable if there is a completely positive trace-preserving map $\mathcal D_{B\to E}$ such that

$$
\mathcal N^c=\mathcal D\circ\mathcal N.
\tag{2}
$$

Writing $P$ for the unassisted private classical capacity, Watanabe calls $\mathcal N$ less noisy when

$$
P(\mathcal N^c)=0.
\tag{3}
$$

Equivalently, Eq. (3) requires that, for every $n\ge1$ and every classical–quantum ensemble on $UA^{n}$, the receiver and environment outputs obey

$$
I(U:B^n)_{(\operatorname{id}_U\otimes\mathcal N^{\otimes n})(\omega)}
 \ge
 I(U:E^n)_{(\operatorname{id}_U\otimes(\mathcal N^c)^{\otimes n})(\omega)}.
\tag{4}
$$

Thus the question asks whether the inclusion implied by Eqs. (2)–(4) is strict.
