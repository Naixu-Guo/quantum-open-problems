Can every bounded-degree local Hamiltonian with unknown interaction support be learned efficiently from copies of its Gibbs state at any fixed inverse temperature?

Fix the locality $k$ and interaction degree $g$ as positive constants. Let $\mathcal{P}_{n,k}$ contain the nonidentity $n$-qubit Pauli strings of weight at most $k$. Consider an unknown Hamiltonian

$$
H=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n,k}}a_P P,
\qquad a_P\in[-1,1],
\qquad
\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}
\bigl|\{P:a_P\ne0,\ j\in\operatorname{supp}(P)\}\bigr|\leq g.
\tag{1}
$$

The nonzero terms are not supplied to the learner. The available data are independent copies of

$$
\rho_\beta:=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H})},
\tag{2}
$$

where the inverse temperature $\beta>0$ is known and fixed independently of $n$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/10$, the output must satisfy $\max_P|\widehat a_P-a_P|\leq\varepsilon$ with probability at least $2/3$. Exact identification of arbitrarily small coefficients is not required.

Can these unknown interactions be learned using $\operatorname{poly}(n,1/\varepsilon)$ copies and computational time at every fixed inverse temperature $\beta>0$?

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2).
