## Source

Xu and Wang pose the question explicitly as Open Problem 2 in Section 5: for a Pauli-periodic gate in a fixed, sufficiently high level of the hierarchy, can the largest Pauli periodicity grow as $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ instead of $\log n$? [XW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.22201). Eqs. (2) and (4) fix a phase-insensitive definition and a precise reading of polynomial growth; the restriction $k\geq3$ excludes the Clifford level, where the answer is known.

## Progress

The classification of diagonal hierarchy gates by Cui, Gottesman, and Krishna places the one-qubit phase gates

$$
R_k:=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(1,e^{i\pi/2^{k-1}}\bigr)\in\mathcal{C}_k(1),
 \qquad k\geq2.
 \tag{5}
$$

Since $R_k^{2^{k-2}}=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$ is not a Pauli operator up to phase while $R_k^{2^{k-1}}=Z$, the gates in Eq. (5) have $m(R_k)=k-1$, and tensoring with identities gives $M_k(n)\geq k-1$ for all $n$. Periodicity therefore grows without bound when the level grows, but this does not address growth in $n$ at a fixed level [CGK17](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.95.012329).

Xu and Wang proved in Theorem 4 that every Clifford gate $U$ with $m(U)<\infty$ satisfies $m(U)\leq\lceil\log_2(2n)\rceil$, with equality attained for every $n>1$; Proposition 9 in Section 3.2 gives explicit saturating $S_X$–CNOT–$H$ strings for $n>2$, and for $n=1$ the phase gate $\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$ attains the bound. Hence

$$
M_2(n)=\lceil\log_2(2n)\rceil\qquad(n\geq1).
 \tag{6}
$$

The proof passes to the binary symplectic matrix $F$ of $U$: $U^{2^t}\in\mathbb{T}\mathcal{P}_n$ exactly when $F^{2^t}=I$, a matrix over $\mathbb{F}_2$ of two-power order is unipotent, and $(F-I)^{2n}=0$ yields the bound; tightness uses regular unipotent elements of $\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{F}_2)$. Because $F$ determines $U$ only up to a global phase, Eq. (6) refers to the phase-insensitive periodicity of Eq. (2); if Pauli membership allowed only the phases $\pm1,\pm i$, the scalar Clifford gates $e^{i\pi/2^{r}}I$ would have periodicity $r-1$ for every $r\geq1$ [XW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.22201).

Since $\mathcal{C}_2(n)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_k(n)$ for $k\geq2$, Eqs. (5) and (6) give the elementary lower bound

$$
M_k(n)\geq\max\bigl\{k-1,\lceil\log_2(2n)\rceil\bigr\},
 \qquad k\geq2,
 \tag{7}
$$

which is logarithmic in $n$ at fixed $k$ [CGK17](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.95.012329), [XW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.22201). In Section 5, Open Problem 2, Xu and Wang ask for the largest Pauli periodicity in a fixed level and whether, for a sufficiently high fixed level, it can be polynomial rather than logarithmic in $n$; Eq. (4) isolates that alternative [XW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.22201).

Ordinary finite order is a different criterion. For $U$ with $m(U)<\infty$, let $o(U):=\min\{r\geq1:U^r\in\mathbb{T}I\}$ be its projective order. Every element of $\mathbb{T}\mathcal{P}_n$ squares to a scalar, so $U^{2^{m(U)+1}}\in\mathbb{T}I$ and $o(U)$ divides $2^{m(U)+1}$; if $m(U)\geq1$, then $o(U)\leq2^{m(U)-1}$ would give $U^{2^{m(U)-1}}\in\mathbb{T}I$, contrary to the minimality in Eq. (2). Hence

$$
o(U)\ \text{is a power of two},
 \qquad
 2^{m(U)}\leq o(U)\leq2^{m(U)+1}.
 \tag{8}
$$

Consequently, Clifford circuits realizing linear-feedback shift registers of period $2^n-1$, which Xu and Wang contrast with Open Problem 2 in Section 5, have $m(U)=\infty$ for $n\geq2$ and do not bear on Eq. (4) [XW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.22201).

## Comment

The open alternative is whether some fixed level $k\geq3$ contains gates whose Pauli periodicity is at least polynomial in $n$ for infinitely many $n$, or whether $M_k(n)=o(n^c)$ for every fixed $k\geq3$ and every $c>0$. At fixed $k$, the available lower bound, Eq. (7), is logarithmic. The exact value in Eq. (6) rests on the group structure and symplectic representation of the Clifford level, which are not available at levels $k\geq3$, since these levels are not groups. Letting the level grow with $n$, as for the gates in Eq. (5), or using large projective order without the two-power structure of Eq. (8), does not meet the quantifiers of Eq. (4). Xu and Wang note that polynomial periodicity at a fixed level could reduce the qubit cost of controlled-jump constructions, although their exact level formula for controlled gates is proved only for Clifford targets [XW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.22201). The level reachable by controlling targets above the Clifford level, Open Problem 1 of Xu and Wang, is the record on [the highest Clifford-hierarchy level of controlled gates with higher-level targets](https://qiqc-op.com/problem/op_e432fc5ff73e63be/). Literature checked through 15 September 2026; no construction with polynomial periodicity at a fixed level and no upper bound excluding it was found.
