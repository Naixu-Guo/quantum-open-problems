Can weak final-layer depolarizing noise make constant-depth two-dimensional Haar-random circuits classically samplable to inverse-polynomial total-variation error?

Let $n=L^2$ qubits occupy an even-sided square grid. From $|0^n\rangle$, apply a depth-$d$ circuit of independent Haar-random nearest-neighbor two-qubit gates. Use the standard repeating four-layer horizontal/vertical brickwork schedule, with $d$ fixed independently of $n$.

Immediately before computational-basis measurement, apply to every qubit the depolarizing channel

$$
\mathcal D_\gamma(X):=(1-\gamma)X+\gamma\operatorname{Tr}(X)I/2.
\tag{1}
$$

Denote the resulting distribution by $P_{U,\gamma}$. Fix any $a>0$, put $\varepsilon=n^{-a}$, and take $\gamma=K\log(n/\varepsilon)/n$ for sufficiently large $n$. A classical sampler receives the gate description $U$ and produces a distribution $Q_U$.

Is there a constant $d_0$ such that, for every fixed $d>d_0$, some constant $K>0$ permits polynomial-time classical sampling with

$$
\mathbb E_U\|Q_U-P_{U,\gamma}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\varepsilon,
\qquad
\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\frac12\sum_x|P(x)-Q(x)|?
\tag{2}
$$

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2).
