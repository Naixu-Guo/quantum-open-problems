Does there exist a finite-dimensional transpose-degradable quantum channel that is not degradable? Let $V:A\to B\otimes E$ be an isometry defining a channel and a complementary channel by

$$
\Phi(X):=\operatorname{Tr}_E(VXV^\dagger),
 \qquad
 \Phi^c(X):=\operatorname{Tr}_B(VXV^\dagger).
\tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) fixes the output space $B$ and environment space $E$. For the transpose $\mathsf T_E$ in a fixed basis of $E$, transpose degradability means that a completely positive trace-preserving map $\mathcal D:\mathcal L(B)\to\mathcal L(E)$ satisfies

$$
\mathsf T_E\circ\Phi^c=\mathcal D\circ\Phi.
\tag{2}
$$

Ordinary degradability instead requires a completely positive trace-preserving map $\widetilde{\mathcal D}:\mathcal L(B)\to\mathcal L(E)$ satisfying

$$
\Phi^c=\widetilde{\mathcal D}\circ\Phi.
\tag{3}
$$

The question is whether Eq. (2) can hold while no map satisfying Eq. (3) exists.
