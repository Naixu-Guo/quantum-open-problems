Alice ($A$), Bob ($B$), and Charlie ($C$) hold one qubit each. Bob is the middle party in the chain $A\!-\!B\!-\!C$. Tracing out one qubit gives the state of the other two, called their marginal. The given two-qubit marginals $\rho_{AB}$ and $\rho_{BC}$ overlap on Bob’s qubit. A three-qubit density matrix $\sigma_{ABC}$ satisfying $\operatorname{Tr}_C\sigma_{ABC}=\rho_{AB}$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_A\sigma_{ABC}=\rho_{BC}$ is a completion. If at least one completion exists, the marginals are compatible.

Each pair tests its state separately on one shared copy per trial. On $\rho_{AB}$, Alice and Bob independently choose measurements from their own finite menus of positive operator-valued measures (POVMs). Their choices are called settings, denoted by $x$ for Alice and $y$ for Bob. They record outcomes $a$ and $b$, respectively. For example, Alice might label a Pauli $Z$ measurement $x=0$ and a Pauli $X$ measurement $x=1$. Both have outcomes $\pm1$, and the labels are arbitrary. For fixed settings, Alice’s POVM effects $\{M_{a\mid x}\}_a$ and Bob’s $\{N_{b\mid y}\}_b$ are positive semidefinite and each family sums to the identity. Given settings $x$ and $y$, the probability of outcomes $a$ and $b$ is $p(a,b\mid x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}[(M_{a\mid x}\otimes N_{b\mid y})\rho_{AB}]$.

In a classical local model, the parties share a random label $\lambda$. Its probabilities $q_\lambda\ge 0$ sum to one and are independent of both settings. Each $\lambda$ selects a pair of deterministic local response functions $f_\lambda$ and $g_\lambda$ mapping each party’s own setting to its outcome. Let $\delta_{u,v}$ equal one if $u=v$ and zero otherwise. A probability table is Bell-local if it admits the mixture in Eq. (1) with the same distribution and response functions for all setting pairs.

$$
p(a,b\mid x,y)=\sum_{\lambda}q_\lambda\,\delta_{a,f_\lambda(x)}\,\delta_{b,g_\lambda(y)}.
\tag{1}
$$

Such mixtures form the convex hull of deterministic local response functions. A linear bound on joint probabilities obeyed by every Bell-local table is a Bell inequality. A state is Bell-nonlocal if some finite menus of local POVMs yield a table outside this hull, violating a Bell inequality.

Bob and Charlie test $\rho_{BC}$ in the same way. For each completion $\sigma_{ABC}$, Alice and Charlie test the unconditional marginal $\sigma_{AC}=\operatorname{Tr}_B\sigma_{ABC}$ obtained by discarding Bob’s qubit. Their measurements may depend on $\sigma_{AC}$.

Are there compatible Bell-nonlocal marginals $\rho_{AB}$ and $\rho_{BC}$ for which every completion $\sigma_{ABC}$ has a Bell-nonlocal end marginal $\sigma_{AC}$?
