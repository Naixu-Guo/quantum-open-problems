Does sparse-oracle quantum linear-system solving require $\Omega(\kappa\sqrt{s}\log(1/\varepsilon))$ queries in the worst case?

Let $A$ be an invertible $D\times D$ matrix with at most $s$ nonzero entries per row and column, normalized so that $\|A\|\le1$ and with singular values at least $1/\kappa$. Access to $A$ is through standard sparse-matrix location and entry-value oracles. An easily prepared normalized state $|b\rangle$ is given.

The task is to prepare a state within Euclidean distance $\epsilon$ of

$$
|x\rangle=\frac{A^{-1}|b\rangle}{\|A^{-1}|b\rangle\|}.
\tag{1}
$$

Is the worst-case number of sparse-oracle calls necessarily

$$
Q_{\mathrm{sparse}}(\kappa,s,\epsilon)
=\Omega\!\left(\kappa\sqrt{s}\log\frac1\epsilon\right)?
\tag{2}
$$

The intended statement is joint in the three parameters, for nontrivial feasible regimes such as $s\ge3$, $\kappa\ge2$, and $0<\epsilon<1/10$, allowing sufficiently large $D$ and adequate entry precision. It does not assert an arbitrarily growing precision lower bound while holding a completely learnable finite oracle table fixed.

Unless otherwise specified, success probability is at least $2/3$, algorithms are uniform, and asymptotic statements concern growing input size.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2).
