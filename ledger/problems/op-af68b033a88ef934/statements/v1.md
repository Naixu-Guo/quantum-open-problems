Is the following estimation task $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard under randomized polynomial-time Turing reductions? Given $X\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$ with independent entries $X_{ij}=(a_{ij}+ib_{ij})/\sqrt{2}$, where all $a_{ij},b_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independently, and $\epsilon,\delta\in(0,1)$, output $z(X)\in\mathbb{C}$ satisfying

$$
\operatorname{Per}(X):=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j,\sigma(j)},
 \qquad
 \Pr_X\!\left[|z(X)-\operatorname{Per}(X)|
 \leq\epsilon|\operatorname{Per}(X)|\right]\geq1-\delta.
\tag{1}
$$

Here $S_n$ is the set of permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The guarantee in Eq. (1) is required for arbitrary $\epsilon,\delta$, with running time measured in $n,1/\epsilon,1/\delta$.
