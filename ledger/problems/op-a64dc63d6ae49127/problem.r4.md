## Source

He, Zhu, Yao, Liu, Li, and Wang prove Eq. (3) for two copies and explicitly conjecture that universal purification is impossible without non-stabilizer resources for every dimension and every number of copies [HZY+26](https://doi.org/10.1103/bdw8-k91v).

## Progress

For two copies the no-go statement is a theorem: for qubits under completely stabilizer-preserving maps and for every odd $d$ under completely positive-Wigner-preserving maps, $F^{\mathcal A_d}_{\delta}(2,s)=1-\frac{d-1}{d}\delta$ for every $0<s\leq1$, so neither deterministic nor postselected classically simulable processing improves the Haar-averaged fidelity [HZY+26](https://doi.org/10.1103/bdw8-k91v).

Numerical scans for

$$
(d,n)\in\{(2,3),(2,4),(3,3),(3,4)\}
 \tag{4}
$$

agree with the single-copy fidelity to within $10^{-6}$ at the sampled noise strengths and success probabilities. The final journal supplement, Appendix F, restricts its qubit scans to $0<\delta<1-1/\sqrt3$, with noise and success-probability steps of $0.01$. These sampled cases in Eq. (4) do not establish a no-go theorem for the full noise interval or arbitrary copy number [HZY+26](https://doi.org/10.1103/bdw8-k91v).

Without the restriction to classically simulable maps, projecting the $n$ copies of a depolarized qubit onto their symmetric subspace is the optimal universal purifier and raises the fidelity above the single-copy value. This unrestricted result does not determine what is attainable with classically simulable maps [CEM99](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.82.4344).

He, Xiong, and Wang report two-copy resource laws for a feasible Haar-averaged fidelity and success-probability pair $(f,s)$ in the purification regime $f\geq\lambda_0=1-(d-1)\delta/d$. Theorem 1 gives an exact linear law in $f-\lambda_0$ for exponentiated purification mana in odd dimensions; the logarithmic mana includes the normalization $-\log_2 s$. Theorem 2 bounds the minimum of $s^{-1}\mathcal R(J^{\mathcal E}/d^2)$ over feasible successful trace-nonincreasing maps by two linear functions of $f-\lambda_0$ for $d=2^m$. Here $J^{\mathcal E}$ is the unnormalized Choi operator and $\mathcal R$ is stabilizer robustness. The bounds coincide for $d=2$ (Appendix D, Eqs. (S79)–(S81)). These preprint results concern depolarizing noise and two input copies; they do not prove Eq. (3) for arbitrary $n$ [HXW26](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.08626).

Historical GitHub report (2026-09-18): Naixu-Guo reported two-copy universal-purification resource bounds from the He–Xiong–Wang preprint. [Pull request #83](https://github.com/Naixu-Guo/quantum-open-problems/pull/83).

Historical GitHub report (2026-09-24): Yuxuan Zhang (GitHub: yuxuanzhang1995) reported a candidate five-copy stabilizer-purification counterexample and a separate four-copy postselection example. [Issue #108](https://github.com/Naixu-Guo/quantum-open-problems/issues/108).

## Comment

The two-copy theorems and resource laws do not settle the archived equality for arbitrary $n$. Zhang’s unrefereed manuscript reports a deterministic five-copy stabilizer protocol with Haar fidelity $325/486>2/3$ at $d=2$, $\delta=2/3$, and $s=1$, as well as strict improvement for every pure target. This reported counterexample is compatible with the two-copy results and lies outside the final supplement’s sampled qubit noise range [Zha26](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22969546). The catalog’s Unsolved designation is retained here; its interpretation and any change remain a separate maintainer-team decision. The completely positive-Wigner-preserving class in Eq. (2) is defined only for odd $d$, so even dimensions above two remain outside the archived formulation.
