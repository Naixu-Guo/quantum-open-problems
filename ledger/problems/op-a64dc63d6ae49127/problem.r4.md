## Source

He, Zhu, Yao, Liu, Li, and Wang prove Eq. (3) for two copies and explicitly conjecture that universal purification is impossible without non-stabilizer resources for every dimension and every number of copies [HZY+26](https://doi.org/10.1103/bdw8-k91v).

## Progress

Yuxuan Zhang gives a counterexample to Eq. (3) for qubits using five copies at $\delta=2/3$. Choose one of the six signed Pauli axes uniformly and measure four inputs in that basis. If all four outcomes match the chosen sign, prepare that Pauli eigenstate; otherwise retain the untouched fifth input. Pauli measurements, stabilizer preparation, retention, discarding, and classical randomization make this a trace-preserving, completely stabilizer-preserving map, including in the presence of reference systems. Its fidelity is at least $11693/17496=2/3+29/17496$ for every pure target. Its Haar fidelity gives

$$
F^{\mathcal A_2}_{2/3}(5,1)\geq\frac{325}{486}
 =\frac23+\frac1{486}>\frac23.
 \tag{4}
$$

Thus Eq. (4) refutes the archived universal equality with success probability one. Discarding extra inputs and independently accepting the output extend the same conditional fidelity bound to every $n\geq5$ and $0<s\leq1$ at this noise strength [Zha26](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22969546), pp. 2–3.

For two copies the no-go statement is a theorem: for qubits under completely stabilizer-preserving maps and for every odd $d$ under completely positive-Wigner-preserving maps, $F^{\mathcal A_d}_{\delta}(2,s)=1-\frac{d-1}{d}\delta$ for every $0<s\leq1$, so neither deterministic nor postselected classically simulable processing improves the Haar-averaged fidelity [HZY+26](https://doi.org/10.1103/bdw8-k91v).

Zhang also gives the four-copy postselection map with Kraus operator $K=|0\rangle\langle0000|$. At $\delta=1/2$, its Haar-averaged success probability is $s=121/1280$ and its conditional Haar fidelity is $547/726=3/4+5/1452>3/4$. This uses the ratio of integrals in Eq. (2): it improves the success-weighted average, not every individual target. The deterministic five-copy construction above has a strict fidelity gain for every target without postselection [Zha26](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22969546), p. 3.

The arXiv version of He et al. reports numerical no-improvement results for

$$
(d,n)\in\{(2,3),(2,4),(3,3),(3,4)\},
 \tag{5}
$$

in arXiv:2504.10516v2, Table 1 [HZY+26](https://doi.org/10.1103/bdw8-k91v). The four-copy qubit assertion in Eq. (5), under that version’s displayed success-weighted fidelity definition, is contradicted by Zhang’s four-copy example; the manuscript does not establish the cause of the numerical discrepancy [Zha26](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22969546), pp. 3–4. These numerical claims do not affect the analytical two-copy theorem or the five-copy counterexample.

Without the restriction to classically simulable maps, projecting the $n$ copies of a depolarized qubit onto their symmetric subspace is the optimal universal purifier and raises the fidelity above the single-copy value. This unrestricted result does not determine the optimal fidelity attainable with classically simulable maps [CEM99](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.82.4344).

## Comment

Solved by counterexample to the universally quantified equality in Eq. (3): the admissible qubit instance in Eq. (4) suffices to refute it [Zha26](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22969546). The cited resolving result is a working manuscript archived on Zenodo; it is not presented as a peer-reviewed journal publication. The analytical two-copy no-go theorem remains unaffected. Zhang’s result does not determine optimal fidelities, the minimum copy number for a deterministic gain for every target, or the odd-dimensional cases. The completely positive-Wigner-preserving class in Eq. (2) is defined only for odd $d$, so even dimensions above two remain outside the archived formulation.
