Can thirty-three quantum levels be encoded in eleven qubits and exactly recovered after an arbitrary error on one unknown qubit? Let $\mathcal P_{11}=\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes 11}$, where the weight of a Pauli string counts its nonidentity factors. Seek a rank-$33$ orthogonal projector $P$ on $(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes 11}$ such that

$$
PEP=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(PE)}{33}P
\qquad(E\in\mathcal P_{11},\ 1\leq\operatorname{wt}(E)\leq 2).
\tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) is the Knill–Laflamme condition for exact correction of one arbitrary single-qubit error. The projector may be any complex code subspace, and the protocol starts without shared entanglement.
