Can arbitrary mixed fermionic Gaussian states be learned to trace distance $\varepsilon$ from $\Theta(m^2/\varepsilon^2)$ copies using no measurements across copies?

Consider $m\geq2$ fermionic modes with Majorana operators $c_1,\ldots,c_{2m}$ satisfying $c_ac_b+c_bc_a=2\delta_{ab}I$, where $\delta_{ab}$ is the Kronecker delta. Let $\mathcal G_m$ be the fermionic Gaussian states, namely the states

$$
\rho=\frac{e^{-K}}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-K})},
\qquad
K:=\frac{i}{4}\sum_{a,b=1}^{2m}A_{ab}c_ac_b,
\qquad A\in\mathbb R^{2m\times2m},\quad A^T=-A,
\tag{1}
$$

together with their limiting states, including pure Gaussian states. For $0<\varepsilon<1/10$, let $N_1(m,\varepsilon)$ be the minimum worst-case copy count for outputting a classical description of $\widehat\rho\in\mathcal G_m$ such that

$$
\Pr\!\left[D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\rho,\widehat\rho)\leq\varepsilon\right]\geq\frac23,
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\rho,\sigma):=\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1.
\tag{2}
$$

Each measurement may act on all modes of one copy and an ancilla. Measurements may adapt to previous classical outcomes, but quantum memory cannot connect different copies. The measurements need not be fermionic Gaussian, and computational efficiency is not imposed.

Is $N_1(m,\varepsilon)=\Theta(m^2/\varepsilon^2)$ for this general mixed-state family?

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2).
