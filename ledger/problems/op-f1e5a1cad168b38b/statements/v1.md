Can loss of one qubit held by an honest party reduce the two-way distillable secret key by an arbitrarily large amount? Let $K_D(A:B)_\rho$ denote the asymptotic secret-key rate obtainable from $\rho_{AB}$ by local operations and public two-way classical communication, with an adversary holding a purification. The question is whether there are finite-dimensional states $\rho^{(r)}_{A_ra:B_r}$, with $\dim a=2$, such that

$$
K_D(A_ra:B_r)_{\rho^{(r)}}
 -K_D(A_r:B_r)_{\operatorname{Tr}_a\rho^{(r)}}
 \xrightarrow[r\to\infty]{}\infty.
\tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) is the $AB$-locking question: the discarded qubit belongs to Alice rather than being transferred to the adversary.
