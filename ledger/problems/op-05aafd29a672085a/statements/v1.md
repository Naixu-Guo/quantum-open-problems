Do there exist two bipartite states, each with zero two-way distillable entanglement, whose tensor product has positive two-way distillable entanglement? Let $\rho_{AB}$ and $\sigma_{A'B'}$ be states on arbitrary finite-dimensional bipartite Hilbert spaces. For any bipartite state $\omega$, let $D_{\leftrightarrow}(\omega)$ denote its asymptotic distillable entanglement, in maximally entangled qubit pairs per copy, under local quantum operations and unlimited two-way classical communication (LOCC). When the two states are combined, Alice controls $AA'$ and Bob controls $BB'$, and the distillable entanglement of the product is taken across the cut $AA':BB'$. No additional entangled state, catalyst, or extra separated party is available. The question is whether some pair satisfies

$$
D_{\leftrightarrow}(\rho_{AB})=D_{\leftrightarrow}(\sigma_{A'B'})=0,
 \qquad
 D_{\leftrightarrow}(\rho_{AB}\otimes\sigma_{A'B'})>0.
\tag{1}
$$
