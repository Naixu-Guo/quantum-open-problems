Do all almost-quantum correlations satisfy information causality? A bipartite conditional distribution $p(a,b\mid x,y)$ with finite input and output sets is almost quantum if there are a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, a unit vector $|\psi\rangle$, and projective measurements $\{P_a^x\}_a$, $\{Q_b^y\}_b$ on $\mathcal H$ such that

$$
P_a^x Q_b^y|\psi\rangle=Q_b^y P_a^x|\psi\rangle,
 \qquad p(a,b\mid x,y)=\langle\psi|P_a^x Q_b^y|\psi\rangle.
\tag{1}
$$

Each measurement consists of orthogonal projections summing to the identity. The commutation condition in (1) is imposed only on the shared state, not as an operator identity.

Alice receives $L$ independent uniform bits $Z_1,\ldots,Z_L$. Bob receives an independent uniform index $J\in\{1,\ldots,L\}$. They may use shared randomness and almost-quantum boxes independent of these inputs. Alice sends Bob an $m$-bit classical message, with no communication from Bob to Alice, and Bob outputs a binary guess $G$ for $Z_J$. Information causality requires

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{L} I(Z_j:G\mid J=j)\leq m,
\tag{2}
$$

where $I$ is Shannon mutual information in bits. Must (2) hold for every finite protocol of this form and all integers $L\geq1$ and $m\geq0$?
