What is the smallest output dimension in which the minimum output von Neumann entropy of quantum channels fails to be additive? For a density operator $\sigma$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space write $H(\sigma)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma\log_2\sigma)$ for its von Neumann entropy, and for a quantum channel $\Phi$ define its minimum output entropy by

$$
S_{\min}(\Phi):=\min_{\rho\in\mathcal D(A)}H\bigl(\Phi(\rho)\bigr),
\tag{1}
$$

where $\mathcal D(A)$ is the set of density operators on the input space $A$ of $\Phi$. A product input is always available in $\Phi\otimes\Psi$, so Eq. (1) gives $S_{\min}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)\leq S_{\min}(\Phi)+S_{\min}(\Psi)$ for any two channels; the phenomenon at issue is the strict inequality

$$
S_{\min}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)<S_{\min}(\Phi)+S_{\min}(\Psi),
\tag{2}
$$

and a pair $(\Phi,\Psi)$ of channels satisfying Eq. (2) is called a violation. Define the output-dimension threshold

$$
d_{\min}:=\min\bigl\{d\in\mathbb N:\ \text{some violation }(\Phi,\Psi)
 \text{ has both output spaces of dimension at most }d\bigr\},
\tag{3}
$$

with input and environment dimensions arbitrary. Determine the exact value of $d_{\min}$ in Eq. (3). The companion constructive target, also open at every output dimension, is a deterministically specified (non-random) pair of finite-dimensional channels with a certified instance of Eq. (2).
