## Source

Hastings settled the qualitative existence of violations of Eq. (2) probabilistically [Has09](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys1224). The threshold formulation of Eq. (3) is derived from the quantitative dimension analysis of Belinschi, Collins, and Nechita [BCN16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1567) together with the recorded absence of any deterministic $p=1$ realization [LLW26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15210); no single source states it as a numbered open problem.

## Progress

Hastings disproved additivity of the minimum output von Neumann entropy with finite-dimensional random channels, so the set in Eq. (3) is nonempty; the construction is probabilistic and yields no explicit pair [Has09](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys1224), as are its later simplifications [BH10](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3210).

Belinschi, Collins, and Nechita computed the exact limiting minimum output entropy of random conjugate pairs, exhibited violations of Eq. (2) with size approaching one bit, and proved that their Bell-state criterion detects a violation at output dimension $183$ while failing almost surely up to dimension $182$ within their ensemble; the bound $d_{\min}\leq183$ in Eq. (3) follows, but the threshold is ensemble-specific [BCN16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1567).

Leung, Lovitz, and Wu proved nonadditivity of the minimum output Rényi entropy for $p\in[0,1/4)\cup(3/4,\infty]$ and numerically improved the ensemble-specific threshold of [BCN16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1567) from $183$ to $182$, stating that the improvement is witness-specific and not a universal threshold over all channels, and that at $p=1$ all arguments remain probabilistic, so no deterministic realization of Eq. (2) is known [LLW26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15210).

Deterministic counterexamples exist only for Rényi orders away from $p=1$: Hayden and Winter gave counterexamples for every $p>1$ [HW08](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4753), Grudka, Horodecki, and Pankowski constructed them for every $p>2$ [GHP10](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.2515), and Derksen and Lovitz constructed them for every $p>1$ from subspaces with high geometric measure of entanglement [DL26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07547v2). None of these produces a violation of Eq. (2) at the von Neumann order $p=1$.

Krohn-Grimberghe certified, with exact rational witnesses, a strict violation of minimum output Rényi-entropy additivity for all $0<p\leq1/22$ for the explicit four-to-three channel pair of Cubitt et al. [KG26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17376), [CHLMW08](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3628): explicit small-dimension violations exist at small Rényi orders, sharpening the contrast with the open $p=1$ case.

Collins and Youn violated additivity of the regularized minimum output entropy through a non-random construction on an infinite-dimensional commuting-operator system and noted that it is not clear the approach works in finite dimensions, leaving Eq. (3) untouched [CY22](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07856).

King proved that unital qubit channels are additive with an arbitrary partner [Kin02](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1500791), so a violation in Eq. (3) at $d=2$ would require both channels to be non-unital; beyond the trivial $d_{\min}\geq2$, reflecting that one-dimensional outputs are additive with every partner, no lower bound is known.

## Comment

The unresolved gap is the full range $2\leq d_{\min}\leq183$ of Eq. (3): the upper bound is the ensemble-specific analysis of [BCN16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1567), the lower bound is the trivial additivity of one-dimensional outputs, and the strongest structural constraint is that a $d=2$ violation needs two non-unital channels [Kin02](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1500791). No deterministically specified pair realizing Eq. (2) at $p=1$ is known in any dimension; every $p=1$ construction remains probabilistic [Has09](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys1224), [LLW26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15210). The sibling extremal question of the maximal violation size $S_{\min}(\Phi)+S_{\min}(\Psi)-S_{\min}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)$ at fixed output dimension is not archived here. The delayed-onset additivity-violation problem poses the orthogonal onset question for a single channel and its tensor powers, so neither record subsumes the other; a complementary record on closed-form nonadditivity of the Holevo capacity and entanglement of formation is currently under review elsewhere.
