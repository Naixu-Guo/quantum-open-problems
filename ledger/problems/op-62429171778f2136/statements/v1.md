Fix $m\geq 3$ distinct full-rank density matrices $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m$ on a space of dimension $d$. A source chooses a label $j$ uniformly and sends $n$ copies of $\rho_j$. The receiver must name one label to rule out. An error occurs precisely when the receiver rules out the label the source chose.

For each $n$, the receiver may measure all $n$ copies jointly. Let $M_i$ be the measurement operator for ruling out label $i$. These operators are positive semidefinite and sum to the identity on the $d^n$-dimensional input space. When the source chooses $j$, the probability of error is $\operatorname{Tr}(M_j\rho_j^{\otimes n})$. The minimum average error over all such measurements is $\varepsilon_n$, as defined in Eq. (1).

$$
\varepsilon_n=\min_{\{M_i\}_{i=1}^{m}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Tr}(M_j\rho_j^{\otimes n}).
\tag{1}
$$

How fast does this optimal error decay as the number of copies grows? Give and prove an exact formula, in terms of the original density matrices, for the lower asymptotic exclusion exponent $E_{\mathrm{excl}}$ in Eq. (2).

$$
E_{\mathrm{excl}}(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\ln\varepsilon_n}{n}.
\tag{2}
$$

The formula may itself involve a minimization or maximization. Once $m$ and $d$ are given, the number and dimensions of its variables must stay fixed as $n$ grows.
