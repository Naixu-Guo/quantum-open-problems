Can every polynomially sparse Hamiltonian be learned with Heisenberg-limited total evolution time when every oracle call has a fixed minimum duration?

Let $H$ be an unknown traceless $n$-qubit Hamiltonian with at most $m\leq n^c$ nonzero Pauli coefficients, where $c>0$ is fixed:

$$
H=\sum_{P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}\setminus\{I^{\otimes n}\}}a_P P,
\qquad
\bigl|\{P:a_P\ne0\}\bigr|\leq m,
\qquad
\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1.
\tag{1}
$$

The supports are unknown. An oracle implements $U_H(t):=e^{-iHt}$ at any chosen real time $t\geq T$, where $T>0$ is an arbitrary fixed constant independent of $n$, $m$, and the accuracy $\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)$. Only forward evolution is supplied; known controls and ancillas may be used between calls, but cannot interrupt a call before time $T$.

The learner must output a sparse coefficient estimate with $\max_P|\widehat a_P-a_P|\leq\varepsilon$ and success probability at least $2/3$. Write $t_{\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_jt_j$ for total oracle evolution time; $\widetilde O$ suppresses logarithmic factors.

Can every such Hamiltonian be learned with $t_{\mathrm{tot}}=\widetilde O(\operatorname{poly}(n,m)/\varepsilon)$ and polynomial query, circuit, and classical-processing costs for every fixed $T>0$?

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1).
