Is it $\#\mathrm{P}$-hard to approximate $\operatorname{ngap}(f)^2$ to relative multiplicative error $a+o(1)$ on a $b$ fraction of uniformly random degree-3 polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_2$? Here $a>0$ and $0<b\leq1$ are constant parameters independent of the number of variables $n$, specifying the relative error and polynomial fraction.

Write the random polynomial in multilinear form as

$$
f(x)=\sum_{i<j<k}\alpha_{ijk}x_i x_j x_k
 +\sum_{i<j}\beta_{ij}x_i x_j
 +\sum_i\gamma_i x_i \pmod{2},
 \qquad x\in\{0,1\}^n.
\tag{1}
$$

In Eq. (1), all coefficients are independent uniform bits. The degree-3 ensemble allows terms of degrees one through three, without conditioning on a nonzero cubic term. A constant term is omitted because it changes only the sign of the gap. Define

$$
\operatorname{gap}(f)
 =|\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:f(x)=0\}|-|\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:f(x)=1\}|,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{ngap}(f)=2^{-n}\operatorname{gap}(f).
\tag{2}
$$

The target is the square of the normalized gap in Eq. (2). An estimate $\widetilde Q_f$ is required to satisfy

$$
\bigl|\widetilde Q_f-\operatorname{ngap}(f)^2\bigr|
 \leq(a+o(1))\operatorname{ngap}(f)^2.
\tag{3}
$$

The $b$ fraction in the question is measured over the coefficient choices for which Eq. (3) holds; $o(1)$ tends to zero as $n\to\infty$.
