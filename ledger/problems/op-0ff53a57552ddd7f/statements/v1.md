Does every unrestricted quantum collision-finding algorithm satisfy the time–space lower bound $T^2S=\Omega(N\log N)$?

Let $f:[N]\to[N]$ be uniformly random, with standard coherent value-oracle access. An algorithm must output distinct $x,x'$ such that $f(x)=f(x')$, with probability at least $2/3$ over the random function and the algorithm. Let $T$ be its number of oracle queries and $S$ its maximum retained workspace, measured in qubits, with classical storage counted as computational-basis memory.

Conjecture: Every such algorithm, without any symmetry restriction, satisfies

$$
T^2S=\Omega(N\log N).
\tag{1}
$$

Together with the collision query lower bound $T=\Omega(N^{1/3})$, this predicts the optimal interpolation

$$
T=\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{N\log N}{S}}\right),
\qquad \log N\lesssim S\lesssim N^{1/3}\log N.
\tag{2}
$$

The oracle itself is external; the algorithm’s query registers, stored inputs and values, and quantum-addressable classical tables count toward $S$. There is no free external table containing the algorithm’s accumulated information. Intermediate measurement records retained for future use also count.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2).
