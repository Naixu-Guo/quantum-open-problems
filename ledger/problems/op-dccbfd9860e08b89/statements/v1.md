Does the Montanaro–Osborne $L^2$ quantum KKL inequality hold for every Boolean quantum observable?

For a Boolean function $f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$ under the uniform measure, define

$$
\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)=\Pr[f(x)\ne f(x^{\oplus i})].
\tag{1}
$$

The Kahn–Kalai–Linial theorem implies, for a universal $c>0$,

$$
\max_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)
\ge c\,\operatorname{Var}(f)\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{2}
$$

The quantum Boolean-function framework and its proposed KKL extension were introduced by Montanaro and Osborne. [Montanaro08](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2435)

In that framework, let $n\ge2$ and let $A$ act on $(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}$, with

$$
A=A^\dagger,\qquad A^2=I.
\tag{3}
$$

Write $\tau(A)=2^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}A$. Let $\mathcal E_i$ completely depolarize qubit $i$ while leaving all other tensor factors unchanged:

$$
\mathcal E_i(A)=\frac{I_i}{2}\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_i A,
\tag{4}
$$

where tensor factors are restored to their original order. Set

$$
D_iA=A-\mathcal E_i(A),\qquad
\operatorname{Inf}^{(2)}_i(A)=\tau\big[(D_iA)^\dagger D_iA\big].
\tag{5}
$$

Conjecture: A universal $c>0$ satisfies

$$
\max_i\operatorname{Inf}^{(2)}_i(A)
\ge c\bigl(1-\tau(A)^2\bigr)\frac{\log n}{n}
\tag{6}
$$

for every such $A$. The particularly clean balanced case imposes $\tau(A)=0$. Jiao et al., Conjecture 1.4, state the displayed variance-weighted form and attribute the underlying squared-influence conjecture to Montanaro and Osborne. It is not a generic statement about bounded observables. [Montanaro08](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2435)[Jiao24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12399)

For a diagonal $A=\sum_x f(x)|x\rangle\langle x|$, one has $D_if=(f(x)-f(x^{\oplus i}))/2$. Squaring gives the bit-flip indicator, so this formulation reduces exactly to classical influence.

The displayed definitions, constraints, and target bounds are recorded in Eqs. (1), (2), (3), (4), (5), (6).
