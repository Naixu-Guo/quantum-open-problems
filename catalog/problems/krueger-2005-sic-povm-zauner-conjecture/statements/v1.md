## Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $d$ | Dimension of the underlying complex Hilbert space |
| $\mathcal{H}$ | A $d$-dimensional complex Hilbert space |
| $\{\vert\phi_i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d^2}$ | A family of $d^2$ normalised vectors in $\mathcal{H}$ |
| $\lvert\langle\phi_i\vert\phi_j\rangle\rvert^2$ | Squared modulus of the inner product (constant for $i\neq j$ for equiangular lines) |
| $p_i$ | Measurement probability $\mathrm{tr}(\vert\phi_i\rangle\langle\phi_i\vert\rho)$ |
| $\rho$ | A density operator (quantum state) on $\mathcal{H}$ |
| $\mathbf{Z}_d$ | The cyclic group/ring of integers modulo $d$ |
| $\vert q\rangle$ | Computational basis vectors, $q=0,\dots,d-1$ |
| $X$ | Shift operator: $X\vert q\rangle = \vert q+1\rangle$ (arithmetic mod $d$) |
| $Z$ | Clock operator: $Z\vert q\rangle = e^{i\,2\pi q/d}\,\vert q\rangle$ |
| $w(p,q)$ | Weyl/Heisenberg operator $Z(p)X(q)$, with $p,q\in\mathbf{Z}_d$ |
| Heisenberg group | Group generated by $\{w(p,q)\}$; also called Weyl–Heisenberg or generalised Pauli group |
| $\vert\phi\rangle$ | A fiducial vector: orbit under the Heisenberg group yields a SIC-POVM |
| $U(d)$ | Group of $d\times d$ unitary operators |
| Clifford group | Normaliser of the Heisenberg group inside $U(d)$ |
| $z$ | A specific order-3 element of the Clifford group acting as $z\,w(p,q)\,z^* = w(q-p,-p)$ |
| SIC-POVM | Symmetric informationally complete POVM (the $d^2$ rank-one operators arising from equiangular lines) |

## Formal statement

The problem is stated in three nested variants, each strictly stronger than its predecessor.

**Problem 1 (SIC-POVMs).** For every dimension $d\ge 2$, decide whether there exists a family $\{|\phi_i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d^2}$ of normalised vectors in $\mathbb{C}^d$ such that
$$|\langle\phi_i|\phi_j\rangle|^2 \;=\; \text{const} \qquad \text{for all } i\neq j .$$
Equivalently, decide whether SIC-POVMs—the POVMs $\{|\phi_i\rangle\langle\phi_i|/d\}_{i=1}^{d^2}$ associated with such equiangular line configurations—exist in every dimension $d$.

**Problem 2 (Covariant SIC-POVMs).** With $X|q\rangle = |q+1\rangle$, $Z|q\rangle = e^{i 2\pi q/d}|q\rangle$ (arithmetic mod $d$), and Weyl operators
$$w(p,q) \;=\; Z(p)\,X(q), \qquad p,q\in\mathbf{Z}_d , \tag{23.1}$$
decide for which $d$ there exists a fiducial vector $|\phi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^d$ such that
$$\bigl\{\,w(p,q)\,|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|\,w(p,q)^*\,\bigr\}_{p,q=0,\dots,d-1} \tag{23.2}$$
is a SIC-POVM (i.e. a group-covariant SIC under the Heisenberg group).

**Problem 3 (Zauner's Conjecture).** Let $z\in U(d)$ be the element of the Clifford group characterised by
$$z\,w(p,q)\,z^* \;=\; w(q-p,\,-p) .$$
Zauner's conjecture asserts that in any dimension $d$, a fiducial vector $|\phi\rangle$ for a Heisenberg-covariant SIC-POVM can be chosen among the eigenvectors of $z$.
